# Incident — 2026-09-26 cross-section duplication and Human Scale tone drift

## Summary

The September 26 edition exposed two editorial-process defects:

1. the same Psyche article was selected in both **Ideas** and **Worth Your Time**;
2. **At Human Scale** selected a serious Jamaican school-access/infrastructure story that was local in geography but too consequential and somber for the intended tonal-reset role.

## Root causes

### Cross-section duplication

Ideas and Worth Your Time were being selected as separate editorial slots without a final edition-wide uniqueness check. The documentation encouraged durable ideas in both places but did not explicitly prohibit the same source piece from satisfying both.

### Human Scale tone drift

The Human Scale protocol correctly required local, ordinary reporting, but its allowance that a story “does not need to be positive” was broad enough to admit a serious access crisis. The process over-weighted **local scale** and under-weighted **low stakes / tonal reset**.

## Corrective actions

- Added a hard cross-section uniqueness gate: one source article, one visible placement.
- Explicitly prohibited Ideas items from being duplicated in Worth Your Time.
- Clarified that Continue Reading is the depth mechanism for a selected Ideas piece.
- Added a Human Scale stakes gate excluding serious hardship, loss of essential services, major infrastructure failure, displacement, safety risk, poverty and similar material-deprivation stories.
- Clarified that Human Scale should normally feel lighter in stakes than the surrounding paper without becoming comedy or forced good news.
- Corrected the September 26 edition by removing the duplicate Psyche Long Read and replacing the school-access item with Calliaqua’s local Culture Pot community event in St. Vincent and the Grenadines.

## Acceptance criteria

Future editions must fail publication if the same canonical source article appears more than once across lead, sections and Worth Your Time.

Future Human Scale selections must pass both:
- the local/ordinary-life test; and
- the low-stakes tonal-reset test.
