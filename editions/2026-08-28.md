## Science

### [Engineered tRNA restored a cystic-fibrosis protein in cells, mice and patient-derived organoids](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aeb0054)

Researchers chemically modified suppressor transfer RNAs and packaged them in lipid nanoparticles designed for lung delivery. In preclinical models, the system helped cells read through premature stop signals in the CFTR gene and restored production and function of the full-length protein.

**Why it matters:** The platform is interesting because nonsense mutations—genetic changes that stop protein production too early—occur across many disorders. But this is not a proven therapy in people: the work is preclinical, and safety, dosing, durability and delivery would all need to survive much more testing before clinical use.

*Science · United States / international · 2026-08-27*

## History & Culture

### [Ancient DNA puts cacao at the Mississippian city of Etowah about 900 years ago](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0353607)

Researchers recovered Theobroma cacao DNA from residues in two pottery sherds excavated at Etowah in present-day Georgia and dated to the 11th–12th centuries. Because cacao cannot grow there, the result points to long-distance movement of cacao or cacao products into the Mississippian world.

**Why it matters:** The finding expands the known geography of pre-Columbian cacao use and suggests exchange networks carried more than prestige objects across North America. The sample is small and the study cannot establish where the cacao originated or exactly how it reached Etowah, so the trade route remains an open question.

*PLOS One · United States / international · 2026-08-26*
