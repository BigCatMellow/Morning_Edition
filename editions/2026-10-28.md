# Morning Edition — October 28, 2026

Israel's unsettled election; Ebola's grim milestone; Cyclone Arnab on India's coast; and a new bid for Europe–Asia cooperation.

## Lead Story

### Israel's election leaves no clear governing majority as votes are counted
Exit polls and early counting after Israel's October 27 election point to a fragmented Knesset. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's allies and the parties opposing him remain short of the 61 seats required to govern, while Arab-led parties may be pivotal. The final tally and any coalition are not yet settled.

**Why it matters:** The election is a test of wartime leadership and Israel's political institutions. A rebuke of the outgoing coalition is not the same thing as a viable alternative government.

## Front Page

### Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 10,000 confirmed cases
The Democratic Republic of Congo's health authorities report 10,060 confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola cases and 4,887 deaths as of October 25. The October 27 milestone comes as health officials investigate imported infections elsewhere in Africa; the latest count is an official surveillance figure, not an independently audited census.

**Why it matters:** The outbreak's scale and high death toll make rapid detection, contact tracing and safe care consequential well beyond the affected provinces.

*Actualité.cd — Congolese reporting on health ministry figures — 2026-10-27*

### Cyclone Arnab reaches India's Andhra Pradesh coast after mass evacuations
Very severe Cyclone Arnab reached the Andhra Pradesh coast on October 28, bringing damaging winds, heavy rain and flooding risk. Local reporting describes tens of thousands of people moved into shelters, while Reuters reports broader precautionary evacuations exceeding 180,000.

**Why it matters:** Preparedness and verified impacts matter more than the storm's category alone: the next test is whether shelters, warnings and relief protect exposed coastal communities.

*The Indian Express — Sreenivas Janyala — 2026-10-28*

### Germany and Singapore expand cooperation as Merz calls for closer EU–Asia ties
At talks in Singapore, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Prime Minister Lawrence Wong agreed to broaden cooperation on industrial AI, research and security. In a separate speech Merz proposed stronger links between the EU and Pacific trade partners; that broader trade pact is a proposal, not an agreement.

**Why it matters:** The visit illustrates how mid-sized economies are seeking more resilient trade and technology relationships amid pressure from larger powers.

*CNA — Nicole Lam, Singapore reporting — 2026-10-27*

## In Case You Missed It

### Recent miss: US EPA proposes weakening oil-and-gas methane controls
On October 27, the US Environmental Protection Agency proposed reconsidering 2024 requirements intended to curb methane leaks from oil and gas operations. The administration projects $45 billion in savings, while critics warn of greater climate pollution; those projected benefits and costs are disputed estimates.

**Why it matters:** Methane cuts can affect near-term warming, and this proposal changes the regulatory incentives to detect and repair leaks. It is not yet a final rule.

*Associated Press — Matthew Brown and Mead Gruver — 2026-10-27*

### Older catch-up: an intact Chimú tomb opens a window into pre-Inca Peru
Peruvian archaeologists announced in September a largely undisturbed funerary platform at Chan Chan containing the remains of 38 people and objects associated with Chimú elites. Because so many comparable tombs were looted, the burial context itself is unusually valuable.

**Why it matters:** The discovery can help reconstruct social rank and ritual in a major Andean civilization on evidence less disturbed by centuries of looting.

*El País América — Renzo Gómez Vega, reporting from Lima — 2026-09-10*

## Outside the Bubble

### An armored amphibian may have lived entirely at sea 237 million years ago
Two exceptionally preserved fossils from China's Guizhou Province led researchers to describe Gerrothorax maritimus, a stem amphibian apparently adapted to a fully marine environment. Fossil location and strontium signatures support the claim; its precise salt-handling physiology remains inferred.

**Why it matters:** The find challenges the apparent evolutionary limit that keeps all living amphibians from spending their entire lives in seawater.

*Nature Ecology & Evolution — Diao, Motani, Jiang and colleagues — 2026-10-26*

## At Human Scale

### On Indonesia's Natuna islands, children tell stories at a library festival
At Natuna's Idrus M. Tahar regional library, the third literacy festival ran October 26–28, bringing together children's storytelling, village libraries, exhibitions and community groups. Local Indonesian-language coverage describes a modest gathering centered on reading and making things together.

**Why it matters:** A small glimpse of island community life, far removed in scale and tone from the day's elections, storms and epidemics.

*ANTARA Kepulauan Riau — Muhamad Nurman — 2026-10-26*

## Ideas

### Can we owe duties to ourselves, not just to other people?
Philosopher Thaddeus Metz argues that self-respect need not be grounded only in autonomy or happiness. Drawing on African philosophical ideas of harmony and vitality, he proposes understanding self-regarding duties as ways of being trustworthy and compassionate toward oneself.

**Why it matters:** The essay provides a serious alternative to treating self-care as either selfish indulgence or merely a means of serving other people.

*Aeon — Thaddeus Metz — 2026-03-13*
