# Morning Edition — October 12, 2026

Djibouti's migrant-boat disaster; emergency rule in Pakistan; Hurricane Simon; Korean missile tests; and a small Italian chestnut festival.

## Lead Story

### Djibouti boat disaster kills at least 139 migrants
A boat with 305 people aboard sank off Obock on October 8–9. By October 11 the IOM and local authorities reported 139 dead, 84 missing and 82 survivors; searches continued.

**Why it matters:** A perilous route to Yemen and Gulf employment remains heavily used despite the region's conflicts.

## Front Page

### Pakistan places Khyber Pakhtunkhwa under emergency rule
President Zardari approved an emergency and governor's rule on October 11 in the opposition-led province. The federal government cites militancy; PTI disputes the takeover.

**Why it matters:** Security measures and constitutional federalism are colliding with democratic representation.

*Dawn — 2026-10-11*

### Hurricane Simon threatens Mexico's Pacific coast
Jalisco issued red alerts and closed some activities as Simon passed close to shore. A late October 11 NHC advisory located it offshore with 105-knot winds; dangerous rain and surf continued.

**Why it matters:** Flooding and landslides may be severe even when a hurricane weakens.

*El Informador — 2026-10-11*

### North Korea fires multiple ballistic missiles
South Korea reported multiple October 12 launches near Pyongyang, with at least one missile traveling over 900 kilometers, after recent military tests on both sides.

**Why it matters:** Reciprocal weapons demonstrations heighten tension and miscalculation risk.

*Yonhap — 2026-10-12*

## In Case You Missed It

### Brazilian state declares emergency as wildfire smoke closes schools
Smoke in Manaus prompted school closures; on October 11 Amazonas reported emergency recognition for 36 municipalities affected by fires.

**Why it matters:** Air pollution turns environmental emergencies into public-health and education disruptions.

*Amazonas Atual — 2026-10-11*

### Older catch-up: climate hazards disrupted school for 171 million students
A September UNICEF analysis found at least 171 million students experienced climate-related school interruptions in 2025 across 106 countries and territories.

**Why it matters:** The long-term cost of extreme weather includes lost learning, especially in lower-income countries.

*UNICEF — 2026-09-10*

## Outside the Bubble

### Webb traces the most distant known fast radio burst
A MeerKAT radio flash was linked with Webb observations to a small galaxy at redshift 2.148, when the universe was roughly three billion years old.

**Why it matters:** Distant bursts can probe otherwise unseen cosmic gas; their engines remain uncertain.

*NASA Webb — 2026-10-08*
