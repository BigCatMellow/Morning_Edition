# Morning Edition — November 10, 2026

A complicated carbon decline, Botswana's diamond ambitions, Germany's remembrance, and the question of intelligence without a brain.

## Lead Story

### Global fossil-fuel emissions edge down, but total carbon pollution still rises
Global Carbon Project researchers estimate fossil-fuel CO₂ emissions will fall 0.4% in 2026, mainly because the Iran war disrupted oil and gas supplies. Coal emissions rose, and emissions from land-use change pushed total global CO₂ emissions up roughly 1%. These are projections, not final annual measurements.

**Why it matters:** A crisis-driven decline is not the same as durable decarbonization; the distinction matters as COP31 begins.

*Associated Press / Global Carbon Project — 2026-11-09*

## Front Page

### Botswana seeks a larger stake in the diamond business that shaped its economy
President Duma Boko says Botswana intends to raise its De Beers ownership to 30% if a proposed consortium acquisition proceeds. The transaction remains proposed, and natural-diamond demand is under pressure from lab-grown alternatives.

**Why it matters:** A resource-producing country is trying to capture more control and value from a major industry rather than merely supply raw materials.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-10*

### Germany marks Kristallnacht amid a rise in reported antisemitic incidents
At a Munich synagogue, Chancellor Friedrich Merz warned that protecting Jewish life remains a test of Germany's democratic commitments. Monitoring group RIAS recorded 8,725 antisemitic incidents last year, though incident reporting is not identical to a complete count of offenses.

**Why it matters:** The anniversary links the memory of state-sponsored persecution to the present obligation to protect minority communities.

*Reuters / RIAS — 2026-11-09*

### Science fiction wins the Booker Prize for the first time
M. John Harrison, 81, won the 2026 Booker Prize for The End of Everything, a short postapocalyptic novel set on the Kent coast. The judges praised its literary ambition and unexpected humor.

**Why it matters:** The award challenges the boundary between literary prestige and speculative fiction, where Harrison has worked for decades.

*The Guardian / Booker Prize — 2026-11-09*

## In Case You Missed It

### A submerged Egyptian palace yields an image of an ancient lighthouse
Archaeologists working near Alexandria recovered a bronze ring engraved with what researchers identify as the Lighthouse of Alexandria. Its image may help compare the ancient monument's representation in different media, although interpretations of small details remain tentative.

**Why it matters:** A small personal object can illuminate how an enormous lost landmark was imagined and circulated in antiquity.

*Live Science / IEASM — 2026-10-14*

### Researchers assemble unusually precise molecular structures that support neurons in culture
Northwestern researchers report giant self-assembled molecular structures with controlled lengths and charge patterns. In cell-culture experiments the materials promoted neuron organization and synapse formation; no human treatment has been demonstrated.

**Why it matters:** Precisely structured materials may eventually help regenerative medicine, but laboratory cell behavior is an early step.

*Northwestern University / primary research — 2026-10-20*

## At Human Scale

### An Alexandria library collects handmade winter items for neighbors
Alexandria Library's Yarn Storm collection began November 9, inviting residents to donate handmade winter accessories through December 2 for Community Lodgings. It is a modest local service project organized around everyday skills.

**Why it matters:** The ordinary work of knitting, giving and sharing a library space deserves a place alongside international headlines.

*Alexandria Library event listing — 2026-11-09*

## Ideas

### Can a creature without a brain be intelligent?
A reported essay follows scientists who study slime moulds that navigate, adapt and form efficient networks. It explores a disagreement between researchers who regard cognition as a broad biological capacity and those who reserve it for nervous systems.

**Why it matters:** The disagreement exposes how definitions can shape experiments and what kinds of behavior scientists count as evidence of mind.

*The Guardian — 2026-09-08*

## Something to Sit With

> What do we live for, if it is not to make life less difficult to each other?

*— George Eliot · Middlemarch*

Dorothea says this while defending the disgraced physician Lydgate against assumptions of guilt. Her question is not detached optimism; it challenges friends to offer practical loyalty before judging a person they know.

**A question to carry:** When has someone made a difficult part of your life easier, and what did that require of them?
