# Morning Edition — October 13, 2026

The IMF sees resilient but lopsided growth; drones endanger Black Sea shipping; a French refrigeration hack exposes physical cyber risk; and a Japanese neighborhood readies its lanterns.

## Lead Story

### IMF raises its 2026 world-growth estimate slightly, but warns the expansion is uneven
The IMF's October outlook projects 3.1% global growth in 2026, up from July's 3.0% forecast. Investment in AI infrastructure is supporting some economies despite high energy prices and the Middle East war, but debt, cyber risks and dependence on a narrow technology supply chain threaten the outlook.

**Why it matters:** The headline number hides divergence: countries supplying AI hardware are benefiting while many energy-importing and debt-stressed economies face weaker conditions. A forecast is not a guarantee.

*Reuters — 2026-10-13*

## Front Page

### Drones strike two more merchant ships near Bulgaria, sinking one
Bulgarian officials say drones hit two vessels off the Black Sea coast on October 13. One sank, with the fate of other crew members still being established; all 15 crew on the Maltese-flagged Chelsea 3 were evacuated, with five treated for minor injuries. Bulgaria says it has not established who launched the drones.

**Why it matters:** The attacks threaten civilian navigation close to a NATO member and follow a similar incident a week earlier. Attribution and the missing sailors' status remain unresolved.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-13*

### Cyberattack remotely warms supermarket refrigerators across France
A cyberattack manipulated refrigeration controls at hundreds of French supermarket locations on the night of October 11–12, forcing stores to discard spoiled food. French reporting describes temperatures reaching 50°C in some cold rooms; authorities are investigating, while equipment maker Danfoss says many affected systems were obsolete.

**Why it matters:** This is a concrete example of software risk becoming physical loss: refrigeration is part of food infrastructure, and insecure controllers can disrupt it without attacking checkout or payment systems.

*Le Monde — 2026-10-13*

### Perseverance becomes the farthest-driving vehicle on another world
NASA says its Mars rover passed 45.22 km on October 11, overtaking the 45.16 km total of Opportunity. Perseverance uses autonomous navigation for most of its driving and is continuing geological work around the rim of Jezero Crater.

**Why it matters:** The distance is more than a record: autonomous driving lets scientists examine more varied ancient Martian terrain, including rocks that may preserve evidence of old water-related environments.

*NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory — 2026-10-12*

## In Case You Missed It

### Economics Nobel recognizes the household-level foundations of stabilization policy
Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford won the 2026 economics prize for work connecting household and business decisions to broader models of inflation, spending and central-bank policy. Their research helped shape how economists analyze shocks and interest-rate guidance.

**Why it matters:** The award illuminates the assumptions behind policies that affect mortgages, jobs and inflation, while leaving room for debate over how well models perform in unusual crises.

*Reuters — 2026-10-12*

### Older catch-up: WMO finds unusually disrupted river flows and shrinking freshwater storage
The World Meteorological Organization's September 17 assessment says 2025 was among the driest years for global river discharge in 35 years. It also documents declining land-water storage and widespread glacier losses, alongside gaps in hydrological monitoring.

**Why it matters:** A water crisis is not only a question of annual rainfall: aquifers, glaciers and seasonal river flows determine what water remains available for people, farms and ecosystems.

*World Meteorological Organization — 2026-09-17*

## At Human Scale

### A Saga neighborhood prepares lanterns for an autumn evening by the castle moat
Akamatsu's community council has announced the 11th Ohori Lantern Festival for October 17, when lanterns will float by the southern moat of Saga Castle. The event is a small neighborhood autumn tradition, scheduled around the moat and nearby park.

**Why it matters:** It is an ordinary example of people making a shared place their own, far from the scale and urgency of the day's major headlines.

*Akamatsu Community Council / Tsunagaru Saga — 2026-10-06*

## Ideas

### Do we overvalue 'knowledge' when reliable belief would serve us better?
Philosopher David Papineau argues that the everyday ideal of knowledge gives too much prestige to apparently direct evidence and too little to strong statistical reasoning. His provocative example contrasts eyewitness testimony with population-level evidence in criminal cases.

**Why it matters:** The essay tests whether we judge evidence by its actual reliability or by how directly it seems to connect us to a fact. Its legal example also exposes important objections about fairness.

*Aeon / David Papineau — 2019-06-03*

## Worth Your Time

### A Record of Breath: what gets saved when a person's papers are cleared away
In an essay for Oxford American, law librarian Marci Wilding visits a decaying former judge's office with an archivist. Their task of rescuing records becomes a meditation on what institutions preserve, what they discard, and what private objects reveal.

**Why it matters:** The piece makes the politics of memory tangible: an official archive can preserve a career while leaving out the ambiguous, intimate objects that complicate a life.

*Oxford American / Marci Wilding — 2026-08-11*
