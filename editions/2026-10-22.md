# Morning Edition — October 22, 2026

A $25 billion privacy ruling; Ebola's cross-border spread; tentative Ukraine diplomacy; and a village of scarecrows.

## Lead Story

### New Mexico judge orders Meta to pay $25 billion over Facebook privacy claims
A state judge imposed a $25 billion civil penalty after a jury found Meta liable for misleading Facebook users about control over their personal information. Meta disputes the ruling and says it will appeal.

**Why it matters:** The unprecedented state-level penalty tests how consumer-protection law can hold major platforms accountable; collection and the final amount remain uncertain pending appeal.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-21*

## Front Page

### Ebola outbreak spreads across borders from Congo
The World Health Organization says the fast-growing outbreak originating in eastern Congo has reached other countries, with Nigeria reporting a case and authorities tracing contacts. Case counts and transmission chains are still changing.

**Why it matters:** Containment depends on rapid detection, protective equipment, cross-border coordination and public trust rather than borders alone.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-21*

### Russia agrees to restart three-way talks on Ukraine, Kremlin says
Following a meeting with a U.S. envoy, the Kremlin said Vladimir Putin agreed to resume talks involving Moscow, Kyiv and Washington. A declared willingness to negotiate does not itself establish a ceasefire or agreement.

**Why it matters:** Diplomatic movement matters only if it produces verifiable changes in fighting and negotiating positions.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-21*

### South Korean officials struggle to guide a shark back to open water
Busan's rescue workers temporarily moved a large dusky shark out of a canal, but it swam back in. Fisheries experts say the animal's behavior and safe relocation remain uncertain.

**Why it matters:** The episode shows the difficulty of managing wildlife in heavily engineered ports without harming an endangered animal.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-22*

## In Case You Missed It

### UN report warns current climate pledges leave world on hotter path
The latest UN emissions assessment finds national commitments remain insufficient to meet international temperature goals, even if implemented as promised.

**Why it matters:** Policy commitments and real-world emissions remain separate measures of progress.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-21*

### Older catch-up: Pompeii's grandest house begins privately funded restoration
The House of Marcus Fabius Rufus, closed since 1980, is receiving a €1.4 million restoration funded by a German entrepreneur. Officials hope to reopen part of the complex to guided groups by Christmas.

**Why it matters:** The project raises questions about conservation financing, public access and how archaeology is presented.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-20*

## At Human Scale

### Waterdown's scarecrow walk becomes an October neighborhood ritual
More than 140 scarecrows made by businesses and volunteers line Waterdown Village in Ontario for the annual autumn walk, which includes a scavenger hunt and a planned pet-and-treat event.

**Why it matters:** A modest community tradition shows how a shared street can become a seasonal project.

*Flamborough Today — 2026-10-22*

### Moral luck challenges our ideas of blame and desert
Robert J. Hartman explores whether two people who take the same reckless risk deserve different moral judgments when chance means only one causes serious harm.

**Why it matters:** The distinction between culpable choices and actual outcomes matters for criminal justice, forgiveness and how we judge others.
