# Morning Edition — September 28, 2026

Oil jumped after the U.S. rejected Iran’s latest Hormuz proposal; Starship began its first orbital mission; Seoul protested Ukraine’s disclosure of two transferred North Korean POWs; and Cicero offers a practical philosophy for acting without pretending to be certain.

## Lead Story

### Oil jumped after the U.S. rejected Iran’s latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz
Brent crude rose above $107 a barrel Monday after President Donald Trump rejected Iran’s seven-day roadmap for reopening the Strait of Hormuz and resuming negotiations. Trump has said talks can continue, while Tehran says reopening the strait depends on U.S. concessions.

**Why it matters:** The diplomatic gap is feeding directly into energy prices and inflation risk. Hormuz remains a critical route for global oil and gas, so disruption reaches far beyond the combatants.

*Reuters / Al Jazeera — 2026-09-28*

## Front Page

### Starship launched on its first orbital mission with an operational payload
SpaceX launched Flight 14 from Texas carrying 26 next-generation Starlink satellites, moving Starship beyond the deliberately suborbital profiles used on its first 13 integrated flights. The nearly 10-hour mission was designed to test orbital insertion, satellite deployment, a deorbit burn and controlled re-entry.

**Why it matters:** Orbital operation is the threshold Starship must cross before it can become a practical heavy-lift system for Starlink and eventually support NASA’s Artemis lunar architecture.

*Associated Press / Spaceflight Now / SpaceX — 2026-09-28*

### South Korea protested Ukraine’s disclosure of two transferred North Korean prisoners of war
South Korea summoned Ukraine’s acting ambassador after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy publicly disclosed that two North Korean soldiers captured while fighting for Russia had been transferred to South Korea. Seoul says the disclosure breached a confidentiality agreement intended in part to protect the soldiers and their families.

**Why it matters:** The transfer creates unusual legal, security and humanitarian questions involving POW law, North Korean resettlement and the Russia-Ukraine war.

*Reuters / Yonhap — 2026-09-28*

## In Case You Missed It

### Recent miss: a study estimates Cold War industrial espionage lifted East German GDP by 7.4%
Researchers using Stasi intelligence archives and digitized company records estimate that stolen Western technological information substantially raised East German productivity, with industrial value added about 22.3% higher in 1988 and economy-wide GDP about 7.4% higher.

**Why it matters:** The work puts an unusually concrete estimate on the economic value of stolen technological knowledge—and helps explain modern export controls, investment screening and industrial counterintelligence.

*Reuters / ROCKWOOL Foundation Berlin — 2026-09-27*

### Older catch-up: researchers warn Himalayan glacier systems are approaching dangerous thresholds
A regional assessment warned that accelerating glacier loss, unstable glacial lakes and an eventual shift beyond ‘peak water’ could reshape water availability and disaster risk across the Hindu Kush-Himalaya region.

**Why it matters:** The near-term problem is not simply less water: melting can first increase floods and unstable lakes before longer-term runoff declines.

*Reuters / regional climate assessment — 2026-09-14*

## Ideas

### Cicero’s scepticism was about calibrated confidence, not doubting everything
Massimo Pigliucci revisits Cicero’s idea of probabilia: beliefs can be strong enough to guide action without being treated as certain. The discipline is to grade confidence, test alternatives and specify what evidence would make you update.

**Why it matters:** It offers a middle position between paralysis and dogmatism: uncertainty is a reason to hold empirical beliefs in proportion to how well they have survived scrutiny.

*Aeon — 2026-09-21*

## At Human Scale

### Vancouver’s first Hakka Youth Fire Dragon festival built its dragon together
A community group in Vancouver’s Chinatown debuted a youth fire dragon assembled collectively from plants, herbs, lanterns, stories and written wishes. The afternoon mixed storytelling, a blessing ritual, tea and the first public dance of the new dragon.

**Why it matters:** It is a small example of a diaspora community turning an inherited ritual into something children and neighbors can physically build together rather than merely watch.

*Hakka House — 2026-09-27*

## Worth Your Time

### It’s not just screen time: children’s media has a pace problem
Early-childhood researcher Natalia Kucirkova argues that minutes alone miss an important design variable: how quickly media forces a child from cue to cue. Even educational material can leave little time to process a feeling, idea or social repair.

**Why it matters:** The essay gives parents and designers a more useful question than simply ‘how much screen time?’: what kind of attention does this piece of media ask a developing child to practice?

*Aeon*
