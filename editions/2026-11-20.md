# Morning Edition — November 20, 2026

Climate diplomacy's difficult final hours; a winter truce proposal; the energy shock; and the science of seeing what is hidden.

## Lead Story

### COP31 draft removes fossil-fuel language as summit approaches deadline
A revised negotiating draft in Antalya drops references to ending fossil-fuel use and an earlier adaptation-finance target. Turkey and Australia say they are seeking an agreement acceptable to nearly 200 countries; critics say the compromise is too weak. The text is not yet a final deal.

**Why it matters:** The negotiations test whether countries can turn climate pledges into a funded, actionable agreement despite deep differences over energy and finance.

*Reuters — 2026-11-19*

## Front Page

### Ukraine and European allies seek a winter pause in attacks on energy infrastructure
After talks in Berlin, Ukraine and European partners called on Russia to halt strikes on energy facilities and allow safer Black Sea food exports. The appeal is a proposal, not an agreed ceasefire.

**Why it matters:** Civilian heating and electricity supplies are exposed as winter approaches, while European participation in negotiations remains a central diplomatic question.

*The Kyiv Independent — 2026-11-19*

### IEA says the Middle East war has permanently changed global energy security
The International Energy Agency's new World Energy Outlook describes an unprecedented oil and gas supply shock linked to the Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz. It expects countries to diversify supply and accelerate some domestic energy investments; the long-term projections are scenarios, not certainties.

**Why it matters:** The disruption affects transport, fertilizer, food prices and energy policy far beyond the Gulf.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-19*

### CERN experiment probes an exceptionally rare particle decay
Researchers at the LHCb experiment report evidence of a rare beauty-particle decay involving two muons. The result so far agrees with the Standard Model rather than establishing new physics.

**Why it matters:** Precision tests of very rare processes can reveal departures from current theory even when the first result confirms its predictions.

*Live Science — 2026-11-18*

## In Case You Missed It

### Recent miss: Ukraine reports attacks on two Russian oil refineries
Ukraine says its forces struck the Ilsky and Afipsky refineries in Krasnodar region, part of a campaign against fuel infrastructure. Independent battle-damage assessments remain limited.

**Why it matters:** The attacks demonstrate the continuing pressure on energy logistics even as diplomatic talks seek a path to a ceasefire.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-19*

### Older catch-up: new imaging method brings faint exoplanets into view
A Nature study published November 4 describes an image-processing method that models stellar glare and can reveal planets otherwise obscured in telescope data. Researchers tested it on James Webb Space Telescope observations; claims about future Earth-like planets remain prospective.

**Why it matters:** Better processing can extract more information from existing observatories without waiting for entirely new telescopes.

*Nature — 2026-11-04*

### Older catch-up: Alaska site offers rare traces of life 13,800 years ago
Excavations southeast of Fairbanks uncovered a hearth, possible shelter features, tools, needles and an antler ornament. Researchers interpret the finds as evidence of repeated occupation; some claims about the earliest dwelling remain provisional.

**Why it matters:** The site helps reconstruct how early communities lived in Beringia beyond the familiar story of migration routes.

*Live Science — 2026-10-20*

## Ideas

### Is moral character more central to identity than memory?
Psychologist Nina Strohminger argues that people's judgments of personal identity track changes in moral character more strongly than ordinary memory loss. Experiments illuminate how people judge continuity, not a definitive metaphysical answer to what a person is.

**Why it matters:** The argument changes how we think about personality, responsibility and the meaning of remaining oneself.

*Aeon / Nina Strohminger — 2014-01-01*

## Worth Your Time

### How intuitions can be rational without being infallible
Philosopher Marc A. Moffett argues that ordinary intuitions can present possibilities and reasons much as perception presents physical surroundings. His account tries to explain why a feeling that something is wrong may count as evidence without being beyond challenge.

**Why it matters:** A careful account of intuition can distinguish useful judgment from overconfidence and prejudice.

*Aeon / Marc A. Moffett — 2026-10-01*
