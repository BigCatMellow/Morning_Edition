# Morning Edition — September 13, 2026

Saudi Arabia’s principal oil bypass around Hormuz is offline, Indonesia searches for about 130 people after a passenger ship overturns, German companies shift first-half investment toward China, and Mercury appears to have hidden part of its geological shrinkage.

## Lead Story

### Saudi Arabia’s East–West pipeline outage has put another major oil-export route at risk

Saudi Arabia’s East–West pipeline remains shut after drone attacks, threatening a route that has been moving roughly 4 million barrels a day from the kingdom’s eastern oil fields to the Red Sea port of Yanbu while traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is badly disrupted. Reuters reports that Yanbu holds only about five to seven days of export stocks at current rates and that repair estimates range from days to more than a month.

**Why it matters:** The pipeline was the principal Saudi bypass around Hormuz. If it stays offline long enough to exhaust Red Sea inventories, traders estimate the loss could reach roughly 4% of global oil supply, deepening an energy shock that is already feeding fuel prices and inflation. The size and duration of the loss are not yet established because Saudi authorities have not published a repair timetable.

*Source: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/saudi-pipeline-outage-threatens-loss-4-global-oil-supply-2026-09-13/) · 2026-09-13 · Saudi Arabia / Gulf*

## Front Page

### Indonesia is searching for about 130 people after a passenger ship overturned in the Java Sea

The Virgo Transport 8, carrying 243 passengers and crew from Surabaya toward Banjarmasin, overturned after reporting bad weather and a severe list early Sunday. Indonesian authorities said 113 people had been evacuated, including 107 survivors, while six deaths were confirmed and about 130 people remained missing as ships and a helicopter searched the area.

**Why it matters:** The immediate issue is rescue, not explanation. The cause remains under investigation, and early accounts have differed on whether the ship fully sank after overturning. Indonesia’s dependence on inter-island shipping makes maritime safety a basic infrastructure issue, but it is too early to assign this accident to a specific regulatory or mechanical failure.

*Source: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/indonesia-passenger-ship-with-243-people-reported-missing-sea-agency-says-2026-09-13/) · 2026-09-13 · Indonesia*

### German companies raised investment in China by a third while sharply cutting U.S. investment

An analysis by the German Economic Institute (IW) of Bundesbank data found that German companies invested €5.6 billion more in China in the first half of 2026 than in the same period a year earlier, while investment in the United States fell by nearly two-thirds to about €4.3 billion. The China figure was roughly in line with the 2020–2025 half-year average.

**Why it matters:** The divergence is a useful counterweight to rhetoric about corporate ‘de-risking.’ German manufacturers still see China as both a large market and a place where they must compete directly with Chinese firms. One half-year flow does not prove a permanent strategic shift, and IW itself argues that subsidies and exchange-rate conditions distort the comparison.

*Source: [Reuters / German Economic Institute (IW)](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/german-firms-lift-china-investment-us-outlays-fall-iw-study-shows-2026-09-13/) · 2026-09-13 · Germany / China / United States*

## Science

### Mercury may have shrunk 10–30% more than scientists had estimated

A Geophysical Research Letters study argues that impact debris has hidden some of Mercury’s contractional ridges and scarps, causing earlier surface-based estimates to miss part of the planet’s long-term shrinkage. After correcting for surface roughness, the team raised its estimate of radial contraction from about 8.3 to 11.6 kilometres and says the true value could still be higher.

**Why it matters:** How much Mercury contracted constrains models of how its unusually large metallic core cooled and how the planet evolved internally. This is an inference from geological mapping, not a direct measurement of the planet getting smaller today; the approaching BepiColombo mission should provide higher-resolution topography that can test the estimate.

*Source: [Associated Press / Geophysical Research Letters](https://apnews.com/article/mercury-shrinking-planet-47b010ee65287f76565bc4859c7c09b3) · 2026-09-10 · Mercury / international research*

## In Case You Missed It

### Record-low Danube water forced Romania to shut both reactors at Cernavodă despite extraordinary attempts to keep cooling water flowing

On August 13, Romania began disconnecting the last operating reactor at the Cernavodă nuclear plant after drought pushed Danube levels too low for reliable cooling; the other reactor had already been shut in late July. Before giving up, authorities detonated a rock obstruction, dredged the riverbed and prepared rock-filled barges to redirect water toward the plant.

**Why it matters:** The episode is an unusually concrete example of climate and infrastructure interacting: a low-carbon power source still depends on local hydrology. Cernavodă normally supplies about one-fifth of Romania’s electricity, and the outage also tightened electricity options for Moldova. It does not show that nuclear power is uniquely vulnerable; it shows why energy-system planning has to account for water as well as fuel.

*Source: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/romanian-nuclearelectrica-starts-shutting-down-sole-working-nuclear-reactor-2026-08-13/) · 2026-08-13 · Romania / Moldova*

## Ideas

### Should moral concepts be judged partly by whether they actually help people solve social problems?

In a review of Matthew Lindauer’s *The Fruitfulness of Normative Concepts*, Joseph Ulatowski examines a proposal to judge concepts such as justice, responsibility and solidarity partly by practical ‘fruitfulness’: whether they motivate good action, prevent bad action, resist bias, build workable consensus and guide behavior. Lindauer argues that moral psychology and experimental philosophy therefore belong inside, not merely beside, normative inquiry.

**Why it matters:** The proposal challenges a familiar division between asking what is morally true and asking what people will actually do. Its attraction is practical relevance; its danger is that a concept could be effective, popular or behavior-shaping without being just. The hardest question is how much practical success should count when it conflicts with moral truth, minority rights or critical independence.

*Source: [Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/the-fruitfulness-of-normative-concepts/) · 2026-07-04 · New Zealand / international philosophy*

## Worth Your Time

### A useful warning against turning every valuable part of life into a tool for producing something else

An Aeon essay argues that modern life increasingly ‘instrumentalises’ activities—friendship, exercise, art, leisure, even sleep—by defending them mainly through the benefits they produce later. Drawing on philosophical distinctions between intrinsic and instrumental value, it argues that some relationships and activities become distorted when their worth is reduced to productivity, health or measurable outcomes.

**Why it matters:** The essay gives a precise vocabulary for a familiar problem: optimization can make even rest and friendship feel like unpaid work. The strongest objection is that instrumental reasons are not inherently corrupting; practical benefits can coexist with intrinsic value. The more defensible claim is that the tool-value should not crowd out the thing’s value in itself.

*Source: [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/instrumentalisation-is-making-everything-a-means-to-an-end) · 2026-02-24 · International*
