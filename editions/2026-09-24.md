# Morning Edition — September 24, 2026

Morocco’s election reshuffles parliament amid weak turnout; a super El Niño could sharply raise heat mortality; a global disease study maps where human-altered landscapes concentrate outbreak risk; and Cicero offers a way to act without pretending to certainty.

## Lead Story

### Morocco’s election reshuffled parliament as turnout fell to 38%
Provisional results put the palace-aligned Authenticity and Modernity Party (PAM) first with 97 of 395 seats, ahead of the RNI on 66 and Istiqlal on 65. The Islamist PJD rebounded from 13 seats in 2021 to 54, while turnout fell to about 38%.

**Why it matters:** The vote changes the balance inside Morocco’s elected institutions after a year marked by youth protests, migration pressure and frustration over jobs and public services. It does not displace the monarchy’s broad constitutional power, and coalition formation still lies ahead.

## Front Page

### A super El Niño could produce hundreds of thousands of excess heat deaths
University of Chicago Climate Impact Lab researchers estimate that extreme heat associated with the 2026 El Niño could cause as many as 451,000 excess deaths worldwide from June 2026 through February 2027, with especially large projected burdens in the Sahel and Southeast Asia.

**Why it matters:** The estimate turns an abstract climate anomaly into a near-term public-health planning problem. It is a modelled projection, not a death count: the useful value is identifying where heat preparedness could matter most, not treating the upper estimate as predetermined.

*Reuters / Climate Impact Lab — 2026-09-23*

### A global study finds emerging-disease risk concentrates where people, livestock and fragmented ecosystems meet
A Nature study assembled more than 58,000 outbreak records across 32 diseases and 169 countries. Outbreak risk was often highest in mosaic landscapes where human settlement and livestock overlap with forests and fragmented ecosystems, while the drivers varied substantially by disease.

**Why it matters:** It argues against a single story in which deforestation or warming alone explains disease emergence. It also shows a major surveillance blind spot: observed outbreaks fall sharply with travel time from healthcare, meaning the global map partly reflects where cases can be detected.

*Nature — 2026-09-23*

### India is moving toward restricting junk-food sales around schools
India is preparing tighter rules for foods high in fat and sugar sold within 50 metres of schools, extending a public-health push aimed at children’s diets and obesity risk.

**Why it matters:** School-food regulation shifts nutrition policy from advice to the physical retail environment around children. The consequential question is how the rule is defined and enforced across millions of schools and nearby small retailers.

*Reuters — 2026-09-24*

## In Case You Missed It

### Colombia moved to bar new mining and oil contracts across its Amazon biome
An Environment Ministry resolution signed in early August designated roughly 483,000 square kilometres of Colombia’s Amazon biome off-limits to new mining concessions and oil or gas contracts, while allowing existing rights to run to their terms.

**Why it matters:** The measure covers an area roughly the size of Spain and makes land-use protection a live test of policy durability across a change of government. Because it is a ministerial resolution rather than a statute, its permanence is less certain than its geographic scale suggests.

*The Rio Times / Colombian Environment Ministry context — 2026-08-07*

## Ideas

### Cicero’s useful middle ground between certainty and indecision
Massimo Pigliucci revives the Academic Skeptic idea of probabilia: beliefs can be strong enough to guide action without being treated as certain. The point is not generalized doubt but calibrated confidence that changes when better evidence arrives.

**Why it matters:** It offers a practical epistemology for decisions made under uncertainty: grade beliefs by scrutiny, separate values from empirical claims, say what would change your mind, argue the other side seriously—and then act anyway.

*Aeon — 2026-09-21*

## At Human Scale

### A northern Ugandan secondary school finally has enough new classrooms and toilets to ease daily queues
Yumbe Secondary School, with about 1,000 students, opened a new classroom block, desks and sanitation facilities this week. Before the project, reporting found one pit-latrine stance serving 109 boys and another serving 82 girls.

**Why it matters:** Its significance is deliberately local: ordinary school infrastructure changes how much of a student’s day is spent waiting rather than learning. After recent stops in Guam, Bogotá and Karnataka, the lens moves to northern Uganda and a practical community-scale problem.

*Daily Monitor — 2026-09-23*

## Worth Your Time

### How tightly should you hold a belief?
Pigliucci’s Cicero essay is worth reading beyond today’s Ideas card because it reconstructs Academic Skepticism as a working method: confidence comes in degrees, scrutiny should scale with stakes, and uncertainty is not an excuse to postpone every decision.

**Why it matters:** It connects ancient epistemology to science, democratic persuasion and personal identity without reducing skepticism to performative doubt.

*Aeon — 2026-09-21*

### A one-tonne Iron Age iron cache reveals trade on a scale archaeologists rarely get to see
A cache of more than a tonne of iron bars found in northern Austria suggests substantial production and shipment along the Danube around 2,200 years ago—enough metal, Nature notes, for roughly 500 swords.

**Why it matters:** Bulk commodities are often archaeologically invisible compared with finished prestige objects. A surviving shipment gives an unusually direct view of the logistics and industrial scale beneath Iron Age economies.

*Nature / Antiquity — 2026-09-23*
