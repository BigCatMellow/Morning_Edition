# Morning Edition — 2026-09-11

The Houthis seize Perim at the mouth of Bab el-Mandeb, Hong Kong jails leaders of its Tiananmen vigil movement, Brazil’s Supreme Court opens a sprawling bank-fraud record, and Italy’s largest steelworks faces a court-ordered shutdown.

## Lead Story

### The Houthis have seized Perim, putting a strategic island inside the Bab el-Mandeb shipping chokepoint under their control

Houthi forces have taken Perim (Mayun) island and the nearby Yemeni town of Dhubab after a rapid advance down the Red Sea coast that also captured Mocha. Perim sits inside the Bab el-Mandeb strait, the narrow passage linking the Red Sea and Suez route with the Gulf of Aden.

**Why it matters:** Morning Edition left Yemen on a return threshold of a verified major territorial shift or a direct effect on Bab el-Mandeb shipping. The capture of Perim clears that threshold. Control of the island does not mean the Houthis can automatically close the strait, but it moves a force that has already attacked commercial shipping onto terrain embedded in the chokepoint itself, while Hormuz is simultaneously under severe pressure.

## Front Page

### Hong Kong has sentenced leaders of the former Tiananmen vigil group to years in prison for peaceful calls to end one-party rule

A Hong Kong national-security court sentenced Lee Cheuk-yan to seven years, Chow Hang-tung to seven years and three months, and Albert Ho to five years and two months after convictions for inciting subversion. Their now-disbanded alliance organized Hong Kong’s annual June 4 candlelight vigils for decades; the case centered on its slogan calling for an end to 'one-party dictatorship.'

**Why it matters:** The sentences put a concrete legal cost on political expression that was once publicly organized in Hong Kong with police permission. Authorities argue the national-security law protects constitutional order; critics argue the case shows peaceful advocacy and commemoration can now be treated as subversion. The significance is institutional rather than only personal: it marks how far the legal boundary around permissible opposition has moved since 2020.

*Source: [The Guardian / Reuters](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/11/chow-hang-tung-lee-cheuk-yan-tiananmen-square-activists-jailed-hong-kong) · 2026-09-11*

### Brazil’s Supreme Court has opened thousands of pages from the Banco Master investigation as the scandal reaches the judiciary itself

Chief Justice Edson Fachin ordered the release of large portions of the Banco Master case ahead of a full-court session on September 15. Newly public material includes Federal Police allegations involving bank executives, central-bank officials and politically connected figures, plus contracts between the bank and the law office of Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes’s wife. The people implicated deny wrongdoing or dispute the investigators’ conclusions.

**Why it matters:** Brazil has moved from a financial-regulation scandal into a credibility crisis for institutions meant to police one another. Disclosure can improve accountability, but released investigative material is not a verdict: allegations, private messages and contracts still need legal testing. The September 15 plenary session is important because the court must decide how to handle claims involving its own members without turning internal conflict into either impunity or trial by leak.

*Source: [Folha de S.Paulo / Reuters](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/poder/2026/09/contrato-de-mulher-de-moraes-com-master-fala-em-consultoria-estrategica-perante-a-pf-leia-a-integra.shtml) · 2026-09-11*

### An Italian court has kept the shutdown order on Taranto’s blast furnaces in place, forcing a choice between industrial continuity and unresolved health risks

The Milan Court of Appeal rejected an attempt to suspend its order requiring the hot end of the former Ilva steelworks in Taranto to close. The plant, Italy’s largest steelworks, directly employs roughly 8,000 people and supports thousands more through contractors; courts have tied continued operation to unresolved asbestos and particulate-pollution risks.

**Why it matters:** This is not simply an environmental ruling or a jobs story. Italy now has to manage a strategic steel asset, a large local workforce and a long-documented public-health burden at the same time. The government is considering public participation and lower-emission production, but an industrial transition is only credible if it can replace both dangerous processes and the livelihoods built around them.

*Source: [Reuters / ANSA](https://www.reuters.com/business/italian-court-confirms-shutdown-large-steelworks-blow-to-meloni-2026-09-11/) · 2026-09-11*

## Science

### Underwater sound from Hunga’s 2022 caldera collapse arrived far ahead of the tsunami it generated

Researchers re-analysed records from 14 seismic stations and concluded that Hunga’s most destructive local tsunami was generated by a sudden submarine caldera collapse rather than by the earlier headline-making explosions. The collapse produced strong hydro-acoustic T-waves detectable as far as about 2,600 kilometres away; those sound waves travel through water much faster than a tsunami.

**Why it matters:** The result identifies a physical signal that could potentially improve warning for volcanic tsunamis, which are harder to monitor than earthquake-generated ones. This is retrospective reconstruction, not yet an operational warning system: automated detection would have to recognize and locate the relevant acoustic signatures reliably in real time before it could be trusted for evacuation decisions.

*Source: [Nature / Science Advances](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02809-6) · 2026-09-10*

## In Case You Missed It

### Zambia closed its highest courts on the final day the opposition could challenge a disputed presidential election

On August 24, Zambia’s Supreme Court complex, which also houses the Constitutional Court, was closed and guarded by police on the last day available for filing an election petition. The judiciary cited security concerns. Opposition leader Brian Mundubile had planned to challenge President Hakainde Hichilema’s re-election after observer reports raised concerns about the vote count; Hichilema denied fraud.

**Why it matters:** Election disputes are supposed to move political conflict from the street into legal institutions. Closing the court at the deadline—whatever the stated security rationale—undermines that channel at exactly the moment it matters most. The episode is more revealing than another tally dispute because it concerns whether a constitutional remedy was practically available.

*Source: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/zambia-closes-courts-last-day-file-election-challenge-2026-08-24/) · 2026-08-24*

### South Korea’s president has reopened the question of replacing the single five-year presidency with a renewable four-year term and a stronger parliament

President Lee Jae Myung said he favors constitutional reform that would allow a president to serve a four-year term and seek re-election while transferring some presidential powers to the National Assembly. South Korea’s current single five-year term dates from the democratic transition and was designed partly to prevent another entrenched presidency.

**Why it matters:** The proposal forces a real institutional tradeoff into view. A renewable term can improve democratic accountability because voters can reward or reject a president, but it can also increase incumbency advantages. Shifting powers to parliament could offset that risk. Any amendment would need broad legislative support and a constitutional process, so this is a reform agenda rather than a change already made.

*Source: [Reuters / Yonhap](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/south-koreas-lee-favours-four-year-presidency-with-re-election-permitted-yonhap-2026-08-14/) · 2026-08-14*

## Ideas

### What if Aquinas’s ethics is ultimately about common happiness rather than each person’s private flourishing?

Joseph Stenberg argues that Aquinas’s ethics has been misread when it is organized around individual happiness. On his reconstruction, law, virtue and grace are ordered toward a shared or 'common' happiness in which individual flourishing is partly constituted by participation in a good common life. Reviewer Angela Knobel accepts the importance of common happiness but questions whether the interpretation is as revisionary as Stenberg claims.

**Why it matters:** The disagreement changes how an old ethical tradition speaks to modern political life. If happiness is fundamentally shared, then justice and law cannot be evaluated only by adding up private benefits; institutions help constitute the good people are able to have together. The strongest caution is historical: other Thomist scholars may already understand Aquinas in substantially this communal way, making the novelty of Stenberg’s reconstruction contested.

*Source: [Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/aquinas-on-the-ethics-of-happiness/) · 2026-04-08*

## At Human Scale

### A Vermont town is packing 2026 into a box for residents of 2076

Warren, Vermont, will bury a new time capsule on Saturday after opening the one residents placed underground in 1976. The Warren Historical Society has gathered ordinary artifacts and messages meant to give people fifty years from now a small record of what daily life in the town looked like in 2026.

**Why it matters:** It is not globally important, which is the point. A town is choosing which ordinary objects, jokes, worries and records of itself are worth handing to people who will live in the same place half a century later.

## Worth Your Time

### Play is not wasted time merely because no one is measuring the outcome

An Aeon essay draws on Schiller, Johan Huizinga and D. W. Winnicott to defend play as a mode of life with intrinsic value rather than a disguised productivity tool. Its central target is the pressure to justify leisure by proving that it improves performance, health or creativity.

**Why it matters:** The essay is worth reading because it asks whether optimization can colonize even the activities meant to escape it. The older virtue term it revives—eutrapelia, the habit of playing well—offers a useful alternative: some activities matter not because they produce a later benefit but because they are part of what a good life already consists in.

*Source: [Aeon](https://aeon.co/essays/play-is-doing-its-work-even-if-were-not-keeping-score) · 2026-09-01*
