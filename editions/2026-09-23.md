## Lead Story

### Tigray’s four-year peace has broken into open fighting across northern Ethiopia

Tigrayan leaders said they have entered a new war with Ethiopia’s federal government as fighting spread into Tigray, Afar and Amhara. Ethiopian Airlines suspended flights to Mekelle, Axum and Shire, while multiple independent reporting streams confirmed clashes and the seizure of airports.

**Why it matters:** This is the threshold recent editions were waiting for: Sunday’s anti-government alliance has moved from political alignment into reported joint combat. The 2020–22 war killed hundreds of thousands; renewed multi-region fighting puts the Pretoria peace agreement and a still-fragile humanitarian recovery at immediate risk.

Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/ethiopian-airlines-suspends-flights-tigray-region-2026-01-07/

## Front Page

### Moldova has declared a 60-day emergency for water and energy before winter

Parliament approved emergency powers beginning September 26 as severe drought depresses the Dniester and Prut rivers while regional wars raise fuel and electricity risks. The government says the measure is preventive, not evidence that supplies are about to fail.

**Why it matters:** Moldova is facing two infrastructure stresses at once: a hydrological deficit affecting drinking water and an energy system still exposed to disruptions in Ukraine and global fuel markets. The emergency lets the government buy and manage supplies faster if those risks converge.

Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/moldovas-parliament-approves-60-day-state-emergency-energy-water-supply-2026-09-22/

### An epigenetic editor silenced hepatitis B DNA without cutting it

Researchers used a disabled Cas9 system to add methyl tags to persistent hepatitis B DNA, suppressing viral activity in human liver cells and mice, with minimal temporary side effects reported in monkeys. A first-in-human trial is already under way.

**Why it matters:** Chronic hepatitis B persists partly because viral DNA can remain inside liver cells for years. Silencing that reservoir without cutting DNA could offer a different route toward a functional cure, but the published evidence is still preclinical and human safety and durability are unknown.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02981-9

### The EU and Philippines have reached the substance of a free-trade agreement

After years of intermittent negotiations, the two sides say the key parameters are settled and will now be converted into legal text. The planned agreement would eliminate more than 94% of tariff lines and broaden access to procurement and investment.

**Why it matters:** The agreement is part of a wider European push to diversify trade toward Southeast Asia. For the Philippines it would replace a unilateral preference scheme with a reciprocal framework, but the pact is not yet formally concluded or in force.

Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/eu-philippines-agree-free-trade-deal-2026-09-22/

## In Case You Missed It

### Rising rich-country bond yields are threatening the debt progress of poorer countries

IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva warned earlier this month that higher borrowing costs in advanced economies can erase hard-won reductions in spreads for developing countries. Senegal is becoming an important test of a revised G20 debt-restructuring process.

**Why it matters:** The global bond selloff is not only a rich-country fiscal story. When benchmark yields rise, refinancing becomes more expensive for countries with far less fiscal room, potentially turning a distant market move into cuts in development spending or another restructuring cycle.

Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/china/imfs-georgieva-says-rising-bond-yields-threaten-progress-developing-country-debt-2026-09-03/

## Ideas

### What disappears when we treat subjectivity as a bug to be engineered away?

Designer Liam Spradlin calls the impulse to make all experience measurable and optimizable “computism.” His argument begins with smart ovens that cannot taste and widens into a critique of interfaces, AI and institutions that substitute behavioral data for the person having the experience.

**Why it matters:** The essay identifies a real design tension: measurement can improve systems, but what is easy to count can quietly become what the system believes is real. The strongest version of the argument is not anti-computation; it asks where quantified proxies stop representing human purposes and start replacing them.

Source: https://aeon.co/essays/if-computers-count-all-the-petals-who-tastes-the-cherries

## At Human Scale

### One village in Karnataka has kept one shared Ganesha for 83 years

Nandagad, in Karnataka’s Belagavi district, still gathers around a single public Ganesha installation at Bazaar Peth, a village-wide tradition begun in 1944 rather than the multiple neighborhood displays common elsewhere.

**Why it matters:** Its significance is intentionally local: a durable way one village organizes a major festival as a shared civic ritual. After recent Human Scale stops in Guam and Bogotá, this moves the lens to a South Asian village and an ordinary community tradition rather than national Indian news.

Source: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/hubballi/83-years-one-idol-nandagad-keeps-ganesh-tradition-alive/articleshow/134358147.cms

## Worth Your Time

### What is it like to be an infant?

Matthew Goldreich takes the philosophy-of-mind question “what is it like to be?” seriously at the beginning of life, comparing psychoanalytic, mentalization and affective-neuroscience accounts of newborn subjectivity. The common thread is an early experiential world organized less by concepts than by raw states of comfort and discomfort regulated with a caregiver.

**Why it matters:** It is a useful bridge between philosophy of consciousness and developmental psychology: before asking when infants know things about the world, it asks what kind of subject there is to do the knowing.

Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjp.70027
