# Morning Edition — October 10, 2026

Washington targets the international court as a former judge receives the Nobel Peace Prize; storms and earthquakes test communities; and quieter pages visit a Spanish village and African ethics.

## Lead Story

### U.S. sanctions the International Criminal Court as former judge Navi Pillay wins the Nobel Peace Prize
Washington imposed institution-wide sanctions on the Hague-based court on October 9, with a six-month wind-down period for affected business relationships. Hours earlier, the Nobel committee awarded its peace prize to South African jurist Navi Pillay for advancing international law.

**Why it matters:** The clash reaches the court's operational finances and its 125 member states, not just the judges already sanctioned. The prize and sanctions point in opposing directions about the authority of international justice.

## Front Page

### Hurricane Isaias struck Florida's Gulf Coast with 105 mph winds
The National Hurricane Center confirmed landfall near Destin at 8:30 p.m. Central on October 9 as a Category 2 hurricane. Widespread outages, fallen trees and flooding followed; hazards continued inland even as the storm weakened.

**Why it matters:** Landfall is only the start of the emergency: inland flooding, damaged roads and prolonged power interruptions can persist after maximum winds fall.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-09*

### A magnitude-7.7 earthquake shook Panama, followed by dozens of aftershocks
A shallow earthquake on October 9 damaged buildings and roads in central Panama. Local reporting recorded damage in Penonomé and other towns; tsunami alerts were lifted. Early injury and damage figures remain provisional.

**Why it matters:** The quake tested infrastructure and emergency response well beyond the epicentral area; revised magnitude estimates and damage assessments should not be mistaken for conflicting disasters.

*La Prensa Panamá — 2026-10-09*

### Trump announces a Russian diesel deal, reversing pressure on Moscow's fuel exports
President Trump said Russia would supply millions of tons of diesel to U.S. and world markets after a call with Vladimir Putin. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy criticized the arrangement as financing Russia's war; deliveries and price effects remain to be demonstrated.

**Why it matters:** A fuel-price intervention also changes the economic pressure applied to a country waging war. The actual contract, sanctions treatment and shipments matter more than the announced tonnage.

*Reuters — 2026-10-09*

## In Case You Missed It

### Taiwan and Eswatini signed a legal-cooperation agreement during a royal visit
President Lai Ching-te hosted King Mswati III in Taipei on October 9; the leaders witnessed a mutual legal-assistance agreement and discussed investment and technical cooperation. Eswatini is among Taiwan's few remaining formal diplomatic allies.

**Why it matters:** The small group of countries maintaining formal ties with Taiwan matters to Taipei's international position, even when a visit attracts little attention abroad.

*Focus Taiwan / CNA — 2026-10-09*

### Older catch-up: Cuba's September grid collapse exposed a deepening energy crisis
Cuba's national electricity system collapsed on September 18 after a transmission failure, leaving millions without power. Independent Cuban reporting and on-the-ground international coverage describe chronic shortages, fragile generating equipment and a U.S. oil blockade aggravating the situation.

**Why it matters:** This is not simply a one-day blackout: recurring national failures reshape access to water, food storage, work and healthcare. The continuing structural causes make the September event worth revisiting.

*Diario de Cuba — 2026-09-18*

## Ideas

### What do we owe ourselves? An African philosophical answer
Philosopher Thaddeus Metz asks whether self-care is more than pursuing happiness or becoming useful to others. Drawing on African philosophical accounts of harmony and vitality, he argues that being dependable, compassionate and truthful toward oneself may be a genuine moral duty.

**Why it matters:** It offers a serious alternative to the familiar account that duties to oneself rest only on rational autonomy, and gives a different way to evaluate self-deception, dependency and personal growth.

*Aeon — Thaddeus Metz — 2026-03-13*

## At Human Scale

### Alcazarén prepares a village fair around an 1837 bandit arrest
The Spanish village is holding its twelfth Luis Candelas fair on October 10–11. Local schoolchildren open the event, neighbors stage a historical reenactment, and visitors find crafts, traditional games, fans and folk music.

**Why it matters:** The event shows a small community turning a particular piece of local history into shared theatre and an ordinary autumn weekend.

*Cadena SER Radio Medina — 2026-10-09*

## Worth Your Time

### Up a Creek: why patient nature writing still matters
David Schurman Wallace revisits John McPhee's reporting on the Colorado River and a new Library of America collection. The essay asks what happens when the timescale of landscapes and the slow work of observation no longer fit the economics of attention.

**Why it matters:** This is a thoughtful account of how the form of nonfiction affects what we can notice, not merely nostalgia for long magazine articles.

*The Baffler — David Schurman Wallace — 2026-10-07*
