# Morning Edition — November 22, 2026

Climate diplomacy in overtime, shipping risks near Hormuz, Europe's budget choices, and a spacecraft at Mercury.

## Lead Story

### COP31 talks stretch into a second day of overtime over fossil fuels
Negotiators in Antalya remain divided over whether a final agreement should explicitly address a transition away from fossil fuels and how wealthy countries will support climate adaptation. A late Turkish draft had not resolved the disagreement by early Sunday.

**Why it matters:** The outcome will indicate whether countries can turn prior climate pledges into clearer collective commitments while preserving consensus among nearly 200 parties.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-22*

## Front Page

### Iran-linked attacks and shipping warnings raise risks near Hormuz
Maritime security monitors reported projectile strikes on tankers around the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, while Iranian state media described additional incidents and military activity. Individual attributions and the precise circumstances of some strikes remain disputed.

**Why it matters:** The strait is a critical energy corridor; even isolated attacks can affect shipping decisions, insurance and supply expectations.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-21*

### EU budget proposal revives digital levy as leaders prepare to negotiate
European Council President António Costa proposed a €1.582 trillion budget for 2028–34, with cuts relative to previous drafts and a possible levy on large digital companies. Member states remain divided over spending and how to pay for it.

**Why it matters:** The seven-year budget shapes agriculture, regional aid, defence and technology investment; its financing requires agreement among member governments.

*Reuters — 2026-11-21*

### BepiColombo enters orbit around Mercury after eight-year journey
The European-Japanese spacecraft stack reached Mercury orbit Saturday, setting up the planned separation of two complementary scientific orbiters in December. The main science campaign is expected to begin in April 2027.

**Why it matters:** Simultaneous orbital measurements should offer a more complete picture of Mercury's interior, surface and magnetic environment.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-21*

## In Case You Missed It

### EU ban on plastic-waste exports to non-OECD countries takes effect
New EU rules prohibit exports of plastic waste to countries outside the OECD, while tightening checks on shipments elsewhere. Regulators aim to reduce pollution transferred abroad and strengthen recycling within Europe.

**Why it matters:** Waste rules change who bears the environmental costs of consumption and whether recycling markets can handle more material domestically.

*Reuters — 2026-11-20*

### Older catch-up: rare Neolithic burial monument uncovered in Saxony
Archaeologists reported a trapezoidal burial enclosure near Döhlen in Germany, roughly 5,500 years old and associated with the Baalberge culture. The discovery was made during work ahead of a pipeline project.

**Why it matters:** The shape and arrangement add evidence about funerary architecture and ritual practices in Neolithic central Europe.

*HeritageDaily — 2026-09-10*

### Fairfax craft show marks its 40th year with community volunteers
The Kiwanis Club of Fairfax recruited volunteers for the City of Fairfax Holiday Craft Show at Fairfax High School on November 21, helping with admissions and visitor logistics to support youth programs. Attendance and proceeds have not yet been verified.

**Why it matters:** The long-running event brings artisans, neighbors and service-club volunteers together around a seasonal local tradition.

Philosopher Sasha Mudd challenges the equation of intelligence with ever-better optimization, drawing on Kant's distinction between instrumental problem-solving and reason's capacity to examine ends and principles.

**Why it matters:** The distinction matters for what AI performance can establish about machine intelligence and what it cannot.

## Something to Sit With

> Nothing is so painful to the human mind as a great and sudden change.

*— Mary Shelley · Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus*

Victor Frankenstein says this while describing the shock of accumulating personal losses. It is a character's response to grief, not a general psychological finding or a detached maxim.

**A question to carry:** Which changes feel hardest because they alter what you expected the world to be?
