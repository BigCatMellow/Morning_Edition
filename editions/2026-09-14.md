## Lead Story

### Bangsamoro’s first parliamentary election is testing whether a decades-long insurgency can finish its transition into self-government

Nearly 2.4 million registered voters in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao are choosing an 80-seat parliament for the first time under the autonomy system created by the 2014 peace agreement between the Philippine government and the Moro Islamic Liberation Front. Voting opened across almost all precincts, but killings in Cotabato City and alleged pre-shaded ballots in Basilan show that the institutional transition remains fragile.

**Why it matters:** The election moves a peace settlement from transitional administration into ordinary electoral legitimacy. The central question is not simply who wins: it is whether former insurgents, rival clans, local parties and national institutions can compete through a parliament rather than through armed power. Results are still incomplete, and election-day violence should not be treated as proof that the process has failed.

## Front Page

### Leading AI labs are beginning to say publicly that frontier capability growth should slow enough for safety work to catch up

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for deliberately pacing gains in frontier-model capability and said Anthropic will give third-party evaluators ongoing employee-like access to its systems. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman endorsed that specific safeguard and said OpenAI would do the same; Elon Musk and Google DeepMind’s Demis Hassabis also backed the general direction.

**Why it matters:** The notable development is institutional rather than the most alarming forecast attached to the debate. Rival labs that normally have strong incentives to move faster are discussing verification, common standards and coordinated pacing. Their risk claims remain contested, and voluntary coordination can also serve incumbent firms, so the test is whether concrete, independently checkable safeguards appear rather than whether executives agree rhetorically.

### Sweden’s centre-left holds a narrow preliminary parliamentary lead, but the final result is still days away

With most election districts counted, the centre-left opposition was projected at 176 seats to 173 for the outgoing right-wing bloc. The Sweden Democrats lost ground, weakening the parliamentary leverage they held during the last term. Late and overseas ballots still have to be counted, and Sweden’s election authority says the final result will not be established until the formal count is complete.

**Why it matters:** The result could change which bloc attempts to form Sweden’s next government, but the more durable signal may be fragmentation: even a confirmed centre-left win would require difficult coalition bargaining. Migration, NATO membership and support for Ukraine also have broader cross-party continuity, so a change of government would not amount to a wholesale policy reversal.

## Science & History

### Liangzhu’s builders used standardized earthworks and water management to make a 5,000-year-old wetland city work

Geoarchaeological analysis of Liangzhu sites in China’s Yangtze Delta reconstructs how builders prepared foundations, used grass-wrapped clay and processed sediment mixtures, and integrated raised mounds with water-management networks. The techniques differ by site but point to learned construction knowledge applied across a difficult, waterlogged landscape.

**Why it matters:** The finding adds physical detail to an old inference about early urban complexity. Monumental architecture does not only show that a society mobilized labor; construction layers can reveal how knowledge, materials and work were organized. The authors infer centralized planning and substantial coordination, though archaeology cannot recover the exact political arrangements behind that organization.

*Nature / Journal of Archaeological Science — [https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02821-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02821-w)*

## In Case You Missed It

### Drought has wiped out most maize and bean production for tens of thousands of households in Central America’s Dry Corridor

A joint Oxfam and Action Against Hunger assessment across 58 municipalities in Guatemala, Honduras and El Salvador found about 91,000 rural households affected by severe drought. Roughly 79% of maize-growing households surveyed reported losing three-quarters to all of their crop, and nearly two-thirds of bean-growing households reported losses of the same scale.

**Why it matters:** The story was easy to miss because the damage is dispersed across farms rather than concentrated in a single disaster. For subsistence households, crop failure rapidly becomes a food, water, debt and migration problem. The figures come from an aid-group assessment rather than a complete agricultural census, so they are best read as a serious regional warning, not an exact measure of every affected household.

## Ideas

### When does protecting health become an unjustified restriction on a person’s own idea of a good life?

A review of T. M. Wilkinson’s The Ethics of Public Health Paternalism examines his case that public-health policy often treats health as if it were identical to wellbeing. Wilkinson argues that some restrictions on alcohol, food or other risky choices may improve health while still making people worse off by their own considered values or by needlessly limiting autonomy; he sees tobacco as an important partial exception because evidence of regret is stronger.

**Why it matters:** The argument forces a distinction that policy debate often collapses: a healthier life is not automatically a better life in every respect. The strongest counterpoint is that Wilkinson may understate mental and social dimensions of health and the extent to which environments manipulate supposedly free choices. The useful question is not whether paternalism is always wrong, but what evidence and moral burden are required before the state removes options for a person’s own good.

*Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews — [https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/the-ethics-of-public-health-paternalism/](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/the-ethics-of-public-health-paternalism/)*

## Worth Your Time

### Re-reading Sartre’s famous existentialism lecture as an argument about responsibility, not a slogan about self-invention

An Aeon essay returns to Sartre’s 1945 lecture Existentialism Is a Humanism and argues that its popular reputation as advice to ‘create yourself’ misses the harder claim. If there is no fixed human essence that determines what we must be, then choices cannot be outsourced to nature, destiny or social roles; even refusing to choose is itself a choice for which the agent remains responsible.

**Why it matters:** The lecture is useful precisely because its freedom is uncomfortable rather than inspirational. Sartre makes agency expansive enough to generate anguish: people choose under constraints they did not choose, and their conduct implicitly presents a way of living as acceptable for others. The strongest objection is that this can exaggerate individual responsibility by underweighting coercion, dependency, disability and social structure.

*Aeon — [https://aeon.co/essays/re-reading-sartres-lecture-existentialism-is-a-humanism](https://aeon.co/essays/re-reading-sartres-lecture-existentialism-is-a-humanism)*
