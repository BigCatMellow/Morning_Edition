# Morning Edition — September 22, 2026

Mali’s army suffered one of its worst recent battlefield losses; Saudi Arabia has restarted its strategic Red Sea oil bypass; Lithuania is designing a power substation for drone warfare; and the Ideas desk asks whether morality can be universal without denying cultural difference.

## Lead Story

### More than 100 Malian soldiers were reportedly killed when JNIM overran a central Mali army camp

Reuters, citing security and military sources, reports that more than 100 Malian soldiers were killed in the September 10 assault on the Dioura camp. Al-Qaeda-linked JNIM has published higher claims and video of captured soldiers; Mali’s army acknowledges losses but has not issued a comparable public toll.

**Why it matters:** The scale suggests a serious deterioration in Mali’s campaign against JNIM despite years of military rule and Russian security support. The exact toll and number captured remain uncertain.

## Front Page

### Saudi Arabia has restarted its East–West oil pipeline, but only at a low rate so far

Saudi Arabia resumed pumping through the East–West pipeline after a shutdown following drone attacks. The line moves crude from eastern fields to the Red Sea and is especially important while Strait of Hormuz traffic remains disrupted. Full throughput has not yet been restored.

### Lithuania is designing a major power substation to survive explosive drones and sabotage

The planned Gižai substation near Kaliningrad will use hardened transformer enclosures, duplicated equipment, anti-tunnelling sensors and other physical defenses. It will anchor the future Harmony Link connection with Poland.

### The EU is moving toward public energy-and-water efficiency labels for large data centres

The European Commission proposed a common rating system for data centres above 500 kW. The label does not cap consumption, but it creates a measurement framework that can later support binding efficiency standards.

## In Case You Missed It

### Webb found chemical evidence that Neptune’s present moons may be debris from an ancient catastrophe

James Webb Space Telescope observations found magnesium-rich clay minerals on several small inner moons and rings. Researchers argue the material likely came from a larger water-altered body destroyed after Neptune captured Triton.

## Ideas

### Can morality be universal without pretending cultures are morally identical?

Susan Neiman reviews Steven Lukes’s attempt to hold two truths together: human moral practices vary enormously, yet describing that variation cannot by itself tell us that every practice is equally justified. The dispute turns on the gap between explaining a norm and giving it moral authority.

## At Human Scale

### Bogotá’s Palenquero community is spending a week bringing its language, food, music and memory into public space

The city’s seventh Palenquero Diaspora Week began September 21 with discussions, performances, food, film and theater organized with the community descended from San Basilio de Palenque. Its stakes are deliberately local: maintaining a language and social memory in the city where the community now lives.

## Worth Your Time

### What is literary criticism actually for?

Colin Burrow uses the shrinking institutional home of English literature as a reason to ask what criticism contributes when it is not merely ranking books or decoding them for specialists. The essay turns the crisis of university English into a broader argument about judgment, attention and expertise.
