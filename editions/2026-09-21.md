# Morning Edition — September 21, 2026

Former enemies in Ethiopia form a new anti-government armed alliance; the global diesel squeeze may persist into 2027; Xi Jinping is headed to Washington this week; and the Ideas desk asks whether more choice really means more freedom.

## Lead Story

### Former enemies in Ethiopia have joined a seven-group armed alliance against Abiy Ahmed’s government

Seven armed groups spanning Tigray, Amhara, Oromia, Afar, Somali and Benishangul-Gumuz announced a coalition aimed at removing Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed’s government. The alliance includes the TPLF and an Amhara Fano movement—forces that fought on opposite sides of the 2020–22 Tigray war.

**Why it matters:** The announcement does not prove that the groups can coordinate militarily, but it links several unresolved Ethiopian conflicts under one political banner. That raises the risk that localized wars become more connected even as old territorial and political disputes remain inside the coalition itself.

Source: Reuters / Addis Standard — https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/seven-ethiopian-armed-groups-announce-anti-government-alliance-2026-09-21/

## Front Page

### The global diesel shortage may last into 2027 as inventories stay unusually thin

Industry data and analysts cited by Reuters show diesel and other middle-distillate inventories under pressure across major markets after disruptions to Middle Eastern and Russian supply. U.S. stock data also show unusually low inventories for the season.

**Why it matters:** Diesel sits underneath trucking, farming, construction and industrial logistics. A prolonged shortage can transmit an energy shock into food and goods prices even when crude-oil prices themselves retreat.

Source: Reuters / U.S. EIA — https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/global-diesel-shortage-likely-last-into-2027-storage-tanks-drain-2026-09-21/

### Xi Jinping will make a state visit to the United States from September 23–25

China’s Foreign Ministry confirmed that Xi will travel to the United States this week for talks with President Donald Trump. The visit follows recent U.S.–China discussions on trade and a proposed channel for AI-related security incidents.

**Why it matters:** The two governments are managing disputes over trade, technology and security while trying to keep competition from becoming uncontrolled escalation. The visit is confirmed; specific outcomes remain prospective.

Source: Reuters / Chinese Foreign Ministry — https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-xi-visit-us-september-23-25-2026-09-21/

## In Case You Missed It

### The United States and Colombia opened the door to joint strikes on Colombian armed groups

In August, U.S. and Colombian officials discussed deeper counter-drug military cooperation after Colombia joined a U.S.-led regional coalition, including the possibility of joint action against armed groups designated as terrorist organizations.

**Why it matters:** The shift potentially moves U.S.–Colombia security cooperation beyond training, intelligence and aid toward direct joint combat operations. Whether that becomes operational policy—and under what legal authorities—remains the important unresolved question.

## Ideas

### An abundance of choices is not necessarily the same thing as freedom

Historian Sophia Rosenfeld traces how modern freedom became closely identified with personalized choice—from consumer goods to careers and identities—and asks whether multiplying options can obscure older ideas of freedom as self-rule, civic power or release from domination.

**Why it matters:** The distinction matters because a society can offer enormous consumer choice while leaving people with little control over the institutions shaping their lives. The harder question is which kinds of choice actually enlarge agency rather than merely expand menus.

Source: Aeon — https://aeon.co/essays/why-an-abundance-of-choice-is-not-the-same-as-freedom

## At Human Scale

### Hundreds of handmade lanterns floated across Tumon Bay in Guam this weekend

Guam’s Nikkei Association held its 12th annual lantern-floating ceremony at Ypao Beach, where residents personalized proa-shaped lanterns with names, drawings and messages before setting them on the water at sunset.

**Why it matters:** Its stakes are deliberately local: a recurring island ritual where Japanese-derived remembrance and Guam’s own language and community practices meet. Recent Human Scale stops included Indonesia, Zimbabwe, Gabon, the Philippines, France, Paraguay, Okinawa and Syria; Guam shifts the lens into Micronesia and the western Pacific.

## Worth Your Time

### Intellectual humility is a virtue—until it becomes a reason not to trust what you know

Philosopher Rachel Fraser uses geneticist Barbara McClintock to challenge the fashionable claim that better thinking always requires more intellectual humility. Sometimes inquiry advances because someone has good reason to remain confident despite widespread disagreement.

**Why it matters:** The essay sharpens a useful distinction between openness to correction and indiscriminate self-doubt. Epistemic virtue may require calibrating confidence to evidence, not simply turning confidence down.
