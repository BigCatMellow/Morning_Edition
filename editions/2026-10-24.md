# Morning Edition — October 24, 2026

Sudan's threatened harvest; Alaska's extraordinary election lawsuit; Indonesia's haze protests; and the science of friction.

## Lead Story

### Sudan's failing rains threaten another famine as war drains food supplies
Farmers across Sudan report severely damaged crops after one of the driest July–September rainy seasons in decades. Reuters' October 23 reporting and food-security monitors warn that drought, war and shrinking aid could combine to deepen hunger during the coming harvest.

**Why it matters:** The harvest was expected to offer some relief after years of conflict. If crops fail while aid rations fall, the consequences reach far beyond one bad growing season.

## Front Page

### Justice Department seeks to halt an Alaska Senate result under a narrow condition
The US Justice Department sued Alaska, asking a federal court to prevent certification if Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan loses by fewer votes than another candidate named Daniel J. Sullivan receives. The case is pending, and the election has not occurred.

**Why it matters:** The unusual request tests federal power over a state's election certification and the boundary between preventing voter confusion and respecting an eligible candidate's place on the ballot.

*Alaska Beacon / James Brooks — 2026-10-23*

### Indonesia's prolonged fire haze draws protests in Central Kalimantan
Students and residents demonstrated in Palangka Raya on October 23, demanding a national disaster response to forest and peatland fires. Smoke has crossed borders, disrupted daily life and raised health concerns across Southeast Asia.

**Why it matters:** The haze is a regional public-health and land-management problem, not just a temporary inconvenience; accountability for burning and effective cross-border cooperation remain contested.

*Associated Press — 2026-10-23*

### Eight foreign ministers urge diplomacy over renewed attacks on Iran
At October 23 talks in Istanbul, Turkey, Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Germany, France, Britain and Italy discussed the Iran war, Yemen and maritime security. Turkish and French ministers warned that fresh strikes could widen the conflict.

**Why it matters:** The meeting shows regional and European governments attempting to limit escalation; statements of agreement are not a ceasefire, negotiated settlement or guarantee that attacks will stop.

*Reuters — Istanbul — 2026-10-23*

## In Case You Missed It

### Recent miss: Russia holds interest rates at 14% as inflation proves stubborn
Russia's central bank kept its key rate at 14% on October 23 and raised its forecast for 2026 inflation to 7.1–7.6%. Domestic fuel costs, public spending and supply disruption complicate attempts to lower borrowing costs.

**Why it matters:** The figures give a measurable view of the economic strain behind the war and the difficult trade-off between inflation control and weak growth.

*RBC / РБК — 2026-10-23*

### Older catch-up: a UN vote challenges the world map many people learned
On September 4, the UN General Assembly voted 164–1 to encourage equal-area map projections, including Equal Earth, after an African-led campaign highlighted how Mercator makes high-latitude regions look too large. The resolution is nonbinding.

**Why it matters:** The issue is both technical and cultural: every flat map distorts something, and the choice of what to preserve shapes how people perceive the world's geography.

*United Nations — Office of the Special Adviser on Africa — 2026-09-08*

## Outside the Bubble

### A glacier model changes how scientists estimate Antarctica's future melt
A NASA-led team found that treating Thwaites Glacier's grounding boundary as a wider zone where seawater penetrates beneath ice improved agreement with past satellite observations. Some model runs projected substantially more sea-level contribution than earlier estimates.

**Why it matters:** The result is a lesson in how a small modeling assumption can matter for coastal planning; it is not a measured doubling of present sea-level rise.

*NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory — 2026-10-20*

## At Human Scale

### Sahagún's San Simón fair brings old trades into a modern village weekend
The León-area town in Spain scheduled its 53rd San Simón fair for October 23–25, with agricultural stalls, local food, craft demonstrations, music and a parade of traditional banners. The report previews the program rather than claiming every event has taken place.

**Why it matters:** For a few days, an ordinary town uses a fair to keep its crafts, food and shared traditions visible to neighbors and visitors.

*iLeón / Sahagún Digital — 2026-10-21*

## Ideas

### An intuition can be a reason without being a verdict
Philosopher Marc A. Moffett argues that an immediate sense that something is wrong can function like perception: it presents a consideration to the mind before explicit reasoning. That does not make every intuition reliable or immune to challenge.

**Why it matters:** The essay asks how rational thought begins when not every reason can first be derived from another belief.

*Aeon / Marc A. Moffett — 2026-10-20*
