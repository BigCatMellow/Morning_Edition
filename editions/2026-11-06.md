# Morning Edition — November 6, 2026

An AI-led market selloff, Japan's fiscal choices, ancient chemistry, and the deep sea's hidden food webs.

## Lead Story

### Global stocks slide as AI investment fears spread across Asia
Asian markets followed Wall Street's sharp November 5 selloff, with Japan and South Korea losing around 6% on Friday. Investors are reassessing expensive AI-linked stocks and the borrowing behind data-centre expansion; market moves do not prove that AI demand has collapsed.

**Why it matters:** The selloff tests assumptions about how much growth and cash flow can justify enormous AI infrastructure commitments.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-06*

## Front Page

### Japan proposes using central-bank asset-sale proceeds for strategic investment
Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi outlined plans to redirect additional proceeds from the Bank of Japan's exchange-traded fund sales toward growth and economic-security priorities. The BOJ says sales will remain gradual and independent.

**Why it matters:** The plan links unusual central-bank holdings to fiscal choices at a time of market stress; it is a proposal, not completed spending.

*Reuters — 2026-11-06*

### Oil market shows early signs of surplus despite Middle East disruption
Reuters reports recovering Gulf exports and weaker demand are easing a previously tight physical oil market. Bank estimates differ and refined-product flows remain disrupted by the Iran war.

**Why it matters:** Price signals and refinery bottlenecks can diverge, complicating inflation forecasts and energy-security decisions.

*Reuters — 2026-11-06*

### Siberian tomb offers disputed clues to ancient cannabis use
Researchers studying a 2,800-year-old Scythian-era burial found cannabis seeds and chemical evidence in preserved hair. The team argues this may show psychoactive use, but a forensic specialist questions whether the hair proves consumption rather than environmental exposure.

**Why it matters:** The study illustrates how archaeological chemistry can extend knowledge of past behavior—and how contamination and interpretation limit certainty.

*Associated Press / Science — 2026-11-06*

## In Case You Missed It

### European flight disruptions follow a wave of false bomb threats
At least 25 commercial flights across Europe were disrupted Thursday by threats that proved false. Investigators are examining possible coordination; purported attribution in some emails has not been substantiated.

**Why it matters:** The incident illustrates the operational cost of threats even when no explosives are found, without evidence to assign responsibility.

*The Guardian — 2026-11-05*

### Older catch-up: a levitated magnet tests an extreme dark-matter hypothesis
Rice University researchers used a suspended magnet to search for interactions with unusually massive dark-matter candidates. They detected no signal but narrowed some possible interaction strengths; the work was presented at a conference.

**Why it matters:** Negative results are part of scientific progress when an experiment rules out previously plausible regions of a search.

*Rice University — 2026-09-01*

### Zhuangzi's challenge to the belief that success is deserved
Philosopher Christine Abigail L Tan examines Zhuangzi's criticism of meritocracy: achievement depends on contingent circumstances and relationships, not simply isolated individual virtue or effort.

**Why it matters:** The essay offers a non-Western framework for discussing opportunity, humility and how societies distribute esteem.

## Something to Sit With

*— William Shakespeare · The Tempest, Act 4, Scene 1*

Prospero speaks after the magical masque disappears. He reflects on the fragility of pageantry, worldly grandeur and human life; the passage is not simply a celebration of imagination.

**A question to carry:** What in ordinary life feels permanent only because we rarely imagine its ending?

## Worth Your Time

### A whale-fall graveyard reveals a deep-sea ecosystem on an immense scale
A Nature News & Views analysis describes research identifying a roughly 1,200-kilometre-long region of accumulated whale remains along the Diamantina fracture zone in the Indian Ocean. The interpretation raises questions about deep-sea nutrient supply and preservation.

**Why it matters:** It offers a rare view of how large animal deaths can sustain ecosystems far below sunlight, and how researchers reconstruct inaccessible habitats.

*Nature / Stephen J. Godfrey — 2026-06-10*
