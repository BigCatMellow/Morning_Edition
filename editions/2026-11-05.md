# Morning Edition — November 5, 2026

Ukraine's aid-shelter toll rises; North Korea tests missiles; a carbon-storage proposal; and the end of a space telescope's long watch.

## Lead Story

### Death toll rises to 42 after Russian strike on Ukrainian aid shelter
Ukrainian police say 42 people have died after Russian bombs hit a school used as a shelter and aid distribution site in Komyshuvakha. Reuters verified police body-camera footage showing events before a second strike; rescue workers continue searching the rubble. Russia says it targeted military units.

**Why it matters:** The reported civilian toll and documented sequence raise urgent questions about civilian protection as US-brokered talks approach. The competing military claims are not independently resolved.

*Reuters — 2026-11-05*

## Front Page

### North Korea reports new air-defence test and cruise-missile drill
North Korean state media says the military tested an air-defence missile and simulated an airport strike with cruise missiles. South Korea independently detected multiple launches; the performance claims remain Pyongyang's.

**Why it matters:** The launches sharpen regional security concerns, but official weapons-test claims should not be confused with independently demonstrated capabilities.

*Reuters — 2026-11-04*

### Australia designates offshore site for proposed Japanese carbon storage
Australia has designated seabed near Darwin for a possible carbon capture and storage project led by Japan's Inpex. Further environmental approvals and licenses are required before injection can begin.

**Why it matters:** The project illustrates a contested climate tradeoff: reducing industrial emissions versus prolonging gas extraction and disturbing marine habitat.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-05*

### NASA's Swift telescope returns to Earth after 22 years
The Swift Observatory reentered near Indonesia after operating about eleven times longer than its planned two-year mission. A proposed robotic orbit-raising rescue failed; Swift had helped scientists study thousands of gamma-ray bursts.

**Why it matters:** The loss closes a productive astronomy mission and highlights the growing importance of orbital maintenance for aging observatories.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-05*

Markets diverged after Democratic congressional victories: major technology firms gained as some investors anticipated slower data-centre expansion, while specialist infrastructure stocks fell. The explanation is analysts' interpretation, not a proven causal forecast.

**Why it matters:** The split reveals how constraints on new computing capacity might benefit incumbents while hurting debt-financed builders.

### Older catch-up: a Gabonese plant enters a debate over medicinal promise and cultural rights
Reporting from Gabon examines iboga, a plant important to Bwiti traditions and studied for possible addiction treatment, as overseas commercial interest grows. Clinical efficacy, safety and sustainable sourcing remain unsettled.

**Why it matters:** The story links medical research to questions of Indigenous knowledge, consent, conservation and who benefits from commercialization.

## Ideas

### Intellectual humility is not always the virtue we think it is
Philosopher Rachel Fraser argues that intellectual traits such as confidence and humility cannot be labeled universally virtuous: their usefulness depends on context, evidence and the work at hand. Barbara McClintock's scientific persistence offers a case.

**Why it matters:** The argument complicates easy advice to always be humble and asks what intellectual habits actually help us find truth.

*Aeon / Rachel Fraser — 2024-10-01*

## Something to Sit With

> I am a part of all that I have met;

*— Alfred, Lord Tennyson · Ulysses*

Tennyson's dramatic speaker is an aging Ulysses who sees his identity as shaped by encounters and travel. The poem's restless appetite for further experience can be inspiring, but it also raises questions about his duties to those he leaves behind.

**A question to carry:** Which encounters have changed how you understand yourself?
