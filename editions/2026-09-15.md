# Morning Edition — September 15, 2026

*Global government borrowing costs reach levels not seen since the financial crisis, Cambodia and Thailand begin a UN-backed maritime conciliation, a Japanese utility abandons two nuclear restart applications after seismic-data manipulation, and a new model asks whether Venus once swallowed a moon.*

## Lead Story

### Global government borrowing costs have climbed to their highest levels since the 2008 financial crisis

The average 10-year yield across the G7 reached 4.285% on Tuesday, while the U.S. 10-year Treasury traded above 5% and Japan's 10-year yield rose above 3%. The immediate pressure combines oil above $100, renewed inflation concerns, expectations of further central-bank tightening and unusually heavy public and private borrowing.

**Why it matters:** Long-term government yields are reference prices for mortgages, corporate borrowing and public debt. A sustained rise does not itself mean a debt crisis, but it steadily redirects government budgets toward interest payments and raises the hurdle rate for investment across the economy. The U.S. Treasury's official September 14 closing curve was 4.97% for the 10-year, so the 5% figure is an intraday market level rather than the previous official close.

Source: Reuters

## Front Page

### Cambodia and Thailand have begun UN-backed conciliation over their disputed maritime boundary

The two countries' foreign ministers delivered opening statements in Singapore to a five-member commission operating under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. Cambodia wants a negotiated maritime boundary or, failing that, a joint-development arrangement; Thailand says an earlier bilateral framework produced little progress and wants a fresh negotiation.

### A Japanese utility has abandoned two nuclear restart applications after investigators found years of manipulated earthquake-safety data

Chubu Electric said its president and chair will resign and it will withdraw applications to restart Hamaoka reactors 3 and 4. An outside investigation found that seismic data submitted during regulatory screening had been manipulated; management pressure to accelerate the long-delayed restart was identified as a contributing factor.

### A new planetary model suggests Venus could have formed a moon and then slowly pulled it back into the planet

Researchers modelled hypothetical moons around slowly rotating Venus and found that tidal interactions would drive them inward rather than outward, eventually producing a collision. No direct evidence shows that Venus actually had such a moon.

## In Case You Missed It

### Myanmar's military offensive around Dawei is tying a stalled port project to the geography of the civil war

Reuters reported on August 21 that hundreds of troops had moved into the Tanintharyi region around the long-stalled Dawei Special Economic Zone, where Myanmar's military-backed government is seeking Russian support for a deep-sea port and power project. Resistance-source claims of burned villages and civilian deaths remain explicitly unverified by Reuters.

## Ideas

### What if gratitude is not mainly a feeling about the past, but an obligation stretched across generations?

A new translation of sixteenth-century Nahuatl dialogues preserves speeches about gratitude, respect and living well among people in and around Texcoco. The review highlights a conception of gratitude that includes repaying earlier generations by doing one's duty for those who will come later.

## At Human Scale

### Young storytellers in Libreville spent a weekend turning the national museum into a place for local-language stories

The Dumwenu association held three days of storytelling at Gabon's national museum, bringing established and younger storytellers together around myths, proverbs and oral traditions told in local languages. Its importance is deliberately local: young people doing ordinary cultural maintenance through storytelling.

## Worth Your Time

### Maybe a statue does not cease to exist when it is crushed; maybe being a statue was only one phase of the same object

A review of Justin Mooney's *Phases of Objects* examines phasalism: the proposal that categories such as statue, sweater or even corpse can be temporary phases of a continuing material object. The hard cases show the price of making one clean identity rule apply to everything.
