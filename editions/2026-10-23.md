# Morning Edition — October 23, 2026

Brazil's razor-close runoff; Taiwan braces for Champi; Saudi oil risk; and the overlooked dogs of Renaissance painting.

## Lead Story

### Brazil's presidential runoff is effectively tied two days before voting
A Datafolha survey conducted October 20–22 places Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro at 50% each of valid votes after rounding. The two-point margin of error makes the October 25 outcome genuinely uncertain.

**Why it matters:** The election will determine the direction of Latin America's largest country; turnout and late decisions may matter more than small polling movements.

*Folha de S.Paulo / Datafolha — 2026-10-22*

## Front Page

### Taiwan closes schools and offices as Typhoon Champi approaches
Local governments announced Friday closures across almost all of Taiwan as the storm approached; forecasters warned of dangerous rain, winds and a possible close pass or landfall.

**Why it matters:** The immediate story is preparedness, not a confirmed damage toll. A storm's track can change materially as it nears an island with densely populated cities and steep terrain.

*Focus Taiwan / Central News Agency — 2026-10-22*

### Oil reaches a five-month high as attacks on Saudi infrastructure intensify
Brent settled at $113.17 a barrel on October 22, up about 4%, after attacks on Saudi energy and civilian infrastructure sharpened supply concerns. Aramco ordered staff out of its Dhahran headquarters.

**Why it matters:** Markets are pricing the risk of disruption; a higher price does not itself establish that the same volume of oil has stopped flowing.

*Reuters — 2026-10-22*

### Supreme Court leaves federal transgender-prisoner care restrictions blocked
The justices declined the administration's request to immediately enforce restrictions on medical care for transgender federal prisoners. The underlying legal case continues; two justices dissented.

**Why it matters:** The decision preserves a lower-court injunction for now, illustrating the importance of administrative-law evidence and procedural posture rather than resolving every substantive question.

*Los Angeles Times — 2026-10-21*

## In Case You Missed It

### Recent miss: social media's role in American news is measurable, not uniform
Pew's October 20 fact sheet finds 40% of US adults regularly get news from Facebook and 34% from YouTube, with substantial differences by platform and demographic group.

**Why it matters:** The survey separates platform reach from the share of each platform's users who treat it as a news source—two statistics often confused in debates over media influence.

*Pew Research Center — 2026-10-20*

### Older catch-up: South Africa's electric-car transition is a contest for future factories
A September 24 report examined a 150% qualifying investment deduction for electric- and hydrogen-vehicle production. Industry executives argue that power, logistics, skills and export access will matter as much as the tax break.

**Why it matters:** The risk is not necessarily immediate plant closures but losing the next generation of vehicle models and the associated supply chains.

*Reuters — 2026-09-24*

## Outside the Bubble

### An astronomical reading of Van Gogh's Starry Night meets an art-historical challenge
Researchers propose that the painting's celestial arrangement resembles the dawn sky of about July 22, 1889. The Van Gogh Museum and an independent astronomer dispute the proposed dating and geometric assumptions.

**Why it matters:** A useful example of science and art history testing one another: a mathematical match is evidence, but not a complete account of an artist's working process.

*The Guardian — 2026-10-22*

## Ideas

### Is intelligence more than getting better at reaching a goal?
Philosopher Sasha Mudd contrasts Hume's account of reason as serving desires with Kant's account of reason as evaluating which ends deserve pursuit. She argues that AI optimisation makes the distinction newly urgent.

**Why it matters:** It asks a harder question than whether machines can solve problems: who gets to choose the goals, and by what standards can those goals be challenged?

*Aeon / Sasha Mudd — 2026-10-01*

## Worth Your Time

### Veronese's dogs change where we look in Renaissance painting
Historian Thomas W. Laqueur follows the dogs in Paolo Veronese's vast religious scenes, showing how animals guide the eye, ground theatrical compositions and complicate easy interpretations of a painter's intentions.

**Why it matters:** A patient, visually specific long read that makes familiar paintings strange again—and shows what close looking can discover without pretending to know the artist's mind.
