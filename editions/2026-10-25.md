# Morning Edition — October 25, 2026

Brazil and Serbia vote; a humanitarian pause is planned in Ukraine; Nigeria's campaign begins; and what a parenthesis can reveal.

## Lead Story

### Brazil votes in a presidential runoff too close for polls to call
More than 158 million Brazilians are eligible to choose between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro on October 25. Datafolha's final October 23–24 survey gave Bolsonaro 51% and Lula 49% of valid votes, within a two-point margin of error. Voting has not yet concluded.

**Why it matters:** The outcome will determine the political direction of Latin America's largest country. In a close race, turnout and the actual count matter far more than a two-point polling lead.

## Front Page

### Serbia elects a parliament after two years of student-led protests
Serbia's October 25 snap election chooses 250 lawmakers. A student-backed movement challenges the ruling Serbian Progressive Party after demonstrations that began with the 2024 Novi Sad railway-station canopy collapse. Polls disagree about the likely winner, and government formation will depend on seats, not just first place.

**Why it matters:** The vote tests whether a sustained anti-corruption protest movement can convert public mobilization into parliamentary power—and whether voters regard the election process as credible.

*Danas — Serbian election guide — 2026-10-24*

### Ukraine and Russia agree on a limited humanitarian pause for Oleshky
Ukrainian Ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets said Ukrainian and Russian negotiators agreed that a local ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky in Kherson would begin October 27. The proposed operation would clear routes, deliver supplies and permit voluntary evacuations. It has not yet begun.

**Why it matters:** For civilians cut off from basic services, a narrow humanitarian corridor could matter immediately; it does not resolve territorial claims, guarantee safe passage or constitute a wider peace settlement.

*RBC-Ukraine — Daryna Vialko — 2026-10-24*

### Nigeria's Tinubu opens re-election campaign amid a cost-of-living squeeze
President Bola Tinubu formally launched the ruling APC's 2027 election campaign in Kaduna on October 24, defending his record and blaming opponents for hunger and poverty. His government's fuel and currency reforms have improved some macroeconomic indicators while imposing steep costs on households.

**Why it matters:** Nigeria's campaign will test whether voters credit long-run fiscal reforms when everyday food, fuel and security concerns remain acute.

*TheCable — Samuel Akpan — 2026-10-24*

## In Case You Missed It

### Recent miss: French demonstrations draw hundreds of thousands, with disputed counts
On October 24, protesters across France demanded relief on living costs and stronger public services. The Interior Ministry counted about 410,000 participants; the CGT union reported more than 800,000. Many marches were peaceful, but clashes and arrests followed in several cities.

**Why it matters:** The different crowd estimates should be attributed rather than averaged. The protests reflect a wider conflict over public spending, education and who bears economic adjustment.

*Le Monde — live local reporting — 2026-10-24*

### Older catch-up: a satellite begins measuring the faint light of photosynthesis
The European Space Agency launched FLEX and Sentinel-3C on September 15. FLEX is designed to measure the weak fluorescence emitted by plants during photosynthesis; Sentinel-3C extends monitoring of oceans, land and ice. The launch and initial contact succeeded, but routine scientific results are still ahead.

**Why it matters:** The mission could eventually help researchers observe vegetation stress and productivity at large scales; an operational promise is not yet a demonstrated scientific finding.

*European Space Agency — mission report — 2026-09-15*

## Outside the Bubble

### A new assessment puts nearly half of reef-building corals at elevated extinction risk
A peer-reviewed reassessment of 892 reef-building coral species finds that nearly half face elevated relative extinction risk. Researchers used coral-cover trends and projected severe bleaching as proxies where species-level population data were missing.

**Why it matters:** The result strengthens the case for treating reef loss as a biodiversity problem as well as a coastal and economic one. Its modeled classifications must not be confused with direct counts of every species' population.

*Nature — Obura, Polidoro and colleagues — 2026-10-15*

## At Human Scale

### Auckland's Sandringham prepares a street festival around its many food traditions
Sandringham Village in Auckland is preparing an October 31 street festival with neighborhood restaurants, cultural performances, school groups, music and family activities. A local reporter spoke with the event manager about the district's changing identity and preparations; the festival has not yet taken place.

**Why it matters:** An ordinary neighborhood gathering can reveal a place through the people who cook, perform, shop and meet there, without needing a national news peg.

## Worth Your Time

### A parenthesis can reveal how a Renaissance writer thinks
Punctuation historian Florence Hazrat traces Philip Sidney's extravagant use of parentheses in his prose and letters. Brackets become more than pauses: they can protect vulnerable admissions, introduce digressions and stage the movement of a thought.

**Why it matters:** A close reading of a small typographical mark opens an unexpectedly rich view of literary style, emotion and the history of how people learned to put thinking on a page.

*The Public Domain Review — Florence Hazrat — 2026-09-30*
