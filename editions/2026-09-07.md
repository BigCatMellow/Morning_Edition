The arrest is a major accountability test, but an arrest and probable-cause finding are not a conviction; the evidence still has to survive trial.

### Germany’s AfD won nearly 44% in Saxony-Anhalt, leaving the anti-AfD ‘firewall’ facing its hardest arithmetic yet

Alternative for Germany won about 44% of the vote and 39 of 83 seats in Saxony-Anhalt, more than doubling its 2021 share and finishing far ahead of the CDU. It is the largest party but lacks a majority. The CDU continues to reject a coalition with the AfD, so forming a government may require parties with deep ideological differences to cooperate.

**Why it matters:** The result is both an electoral landslide and an institutional stress test. Germany’s mainstream parties have relied on refusing coalition cooperation with the AfD; that strategy becomes harder to sustain politically when the excluded party is by far the largest bloc. The immediate question is not whether the AfD ‘won government’—it did not—but whether coalition practice or national CDU strategy changes under the pressure.

### Iran says it will create a new restricted zone around Gulf shipping as Hormuz traffic falls and oil prices climb

Iran says it plans to publish maps for a new restricted zone and shipping corridor around the Strait of Hormuz after another weekend of U.S.-Iran strikes on vessels. Shipping through the strait has fallen to its lowest level since May, while Brent crude traded near six-week highs around $97 a barrel. Iran has not yet published the zone’s exact boundaries or demonstrated how it would be enforced.

**Why it matters:** The Strait of Hormuz is a global energy chokepoint, so even partial disruption affects freight, insurance, fuel costs and inflation far beyond the Gulf. The important distinction is between a threatened or announced restriction and an actual closure: the former is already moving markets, while the latter would be a much larger event. This also connects a regional war directly to central-bank decisions through energy prices.

### Jamaica is trying to move slavery reparations from diplomacy into the surviving legal machinery of the British Empire

Jamaica formally petitioned King Charles III to refer legal questions about Britain’s responsibility for slavery and reparative justice to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council. The king does not decide the request personally and is expected to act on British government advice. The British government continues to oppose reparations.

**Why it matters:** The petition is unusual because it asks an institution inherited from empire to examine legal questions about the harms of empire. A referral would not itself award compensation, but it could turn a political and historical dispute into a judicial one and create a model other Caribbean governments could examine.

## Society & Human Behavior

### Moral language appears to help online engagement only up to a point; beyond that, ‘overmoralization’ is associated with less engagement

A Nature Human Behaviour study analysed more than 1.6 million posts across Twitter, Reddit and 8chan and found a non-linear pattern: posts with more moral content generally attracted more engagement, but very high concentrations of moral language were associated with lower engagement. The result held across 13 sociopolitical topics.

**Why it matters:** Online discourse is often described as if increasingly moral or outraged language always wins attention. This study suggests the relationship may have limits. But it is observational: it shows a robust association in historical platform data, not that adding or removing moral words will causally raise engagement in a particular post.

[Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02560-y)

### Telling patients that symptoms are ‘normal’ can unintentionally make treatment seem unnecessary

Across 14 studies involving 9,371 participants, researchers found that normalizing symptoms could reduce people’s stated inclination to seek treatment because they inferred that the provider was signaling treatment was not warranted. Adding explicit pro-treatment cues could counter the effect. The experiments were conducted online rather than in clinical settings.

**Why it matters:** Reassurance can fail not because the factual message is wrong but because the listener infers a second message the speaker did not intend. That makes this a communication-design problem relevant to medicine and other settings where ‘common’ or ‘normal’ can be mistaken for ‘nothing should be done.’ The clinical importance remains uncertain until the effect is tested against real treatment behavior.

[Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02542-0)

## Ideas

### A sixteenth-century Nahua text treats gratitude as an obligation stretching backward and forward across generations

Alejandro Santana’s review of a new translation of the Bancroft Dialogues describes a Nahua conception of gratitude that is broader than thanking an individual benefactor. The dialogues connect gratitude to respect, social duty and repayment across generations: people inherit the work that made their lives possible and owe something to those who come after them.

**Why it matters:** The idea changes gratitude from a private feeling into a theory of social inheritance. It raises a harder question than ‘what am I thankful for?’: what obligations follow from benefiting from institutions, families, cultures and environments built by people you never met? The colonial setting also complicates interpretation because Christian influence and pre-contact Nahua thought are difficult to cleanly separate.

[Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/pablo-of-texcoco-how-to-be-grateful-an-aztec-guide-to-the-art-of-gratitude/)

### When is stereotyping a useful cognitive shortcut, and when does it become a moral failure?

An Aeon Philosophy Prize essay argues that humans cannot understand the world without generalizing from limited experience, yet those same cognitive shortcuts become morally dangerous when they are allowed to override the individuality and autonomy of other people. The challenge is not to eliminate generalization but to keep testing it against new evidence and resist treating a category as a complete description of a person.

**Why it’s worth your time:** The essay avoids two easy positions: that stereotypes are simply irrational errors, or that statistically useful generalizations justify person-level judgments. Its value is the distinction between an unavoidable cognitive need to compress experience and a moral obligation to remain open to evidence about the individual in front of you.
