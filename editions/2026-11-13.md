# Morning Edition — November 13, 2026

Taiwan's unannounced naval exercises, Congo's Ebola emergency, Hera at its target, and the social meaning of awkwardness.

## Lead Story

### Taiwan reports large unannounced Chinese naval drills across the western Pacific
Taiwanese officials say more than 100 Chinese naval and coast-guard vessels participated in early-November exercises, including operations that appeared designed to restrict outside access. Beijing described carrier activity as routine and did not acknowledge a named exercise.

**Why it matters:** The scale and coordination raise questions about maritime access and crisis signaling ahead of the APEC summit, without establishing that an attack is imminent.

## Front Page

### Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 11,000 reported cases
Congolese authorities report 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths in the Bundibugyo outbreak. WHO's earlier November 3 situation report documented 10,668 cases, showing the outbreak's continuing growth amid conflict and difficult contact tracing.

**Why it matters:** The public-health challenge is not only medical: access, trust and safe monitoring of exposed people are essential to containment.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-11*

### Iran says nuclear monitoring cannot resume under military threats
Iran told the IAEA that restoring inspections is impossible while threats of new attacks continue. The agency says its access to damaged sites and verification of highly enriched uranium remain inadequate.

**Why it matters:** An expanding information gap makes nuclear risk harder to assess independently, even as both sides dispute the conditions for inspections.

*Associated Press — 2026-11-13*

### Europe's Hera spacecraft begins close study of an asteroid NASA struck
ESA's Hera mission has reached the Didymos-Dimorphos system and begun inspecting the aftermath of NASA's 2022 DART collision. Its instruments will investigate how the impact changed the moonlet and dispersed debris.

**Why it matters:** The measurements can help turn one successful asteroid-deflection demonstration into a better-understood planetary-defense technique.

*Associated Press / European Space Agency — 2026-11-12*

## In Case You Missed It

### Brazil's inflation stays above target despite a softer October reading
Brazil's October annual inflation rate was 4.63%, up slightly from September but below economists' median forecast. The central bank continues to face high borrowing costs and pressure to reduce inflation.

**Why it matters:** A modestly better monthly surprise does not erase the underlying policy tradeoff between prices, growth and debt costs.

*Reuters / IBGE — 2026-11-12*

### Older catch-up: an ancient Maya city emerges from Yucatán forest
An archaeological team reported a previously undocumented Maya urban center called Minanbé, with monuments, palatial buildings and a pyramid. The site had remained difficult to access through dense vegetation.

**Why it matters:** A major settlement hidden in plain sight is a reminder of how incomplete our maps of ancient population and infrastructure remain.

*Archaeology Magazine — 2026-09-01*

## Ideas

### Awkwardness may be a shared social problem, not a personal defect
Philosopher Alexandra Plakias argues that awkward moments expose clashes between social expectations and can suppress necessary conversations. The problem is not simply that some people lack social skill.

**Why it matters:** Treating discomfort as a feature of social norms rather than an individual's failure can change when people decide to speak.

*Aeon / Alexandra Plakias — 2024-01-01*

## Something to Sit With

> The best way of avenging thyself is not to become like [the wrong-doer].

*— Marcus Aurelius · Meditations, Book VI, section 6 · translated by George Long*

In this Stoic meditation, a response to wrongdoing is judged by the character it forms in the person responding, not by how closely it mirrors the original injury.

**A question to carry:** Which response preserves the standards you want to live by?
