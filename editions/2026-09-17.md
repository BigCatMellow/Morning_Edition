# Morning Edition — September 17, 2026

UN investigators say there are reasonable grounds to believe U.S. strikes in Iran amounted to war crimes while also finding crimes against humanity by Iranian authorities; South Sudan changes election leadership three months before its first planned national vote; South Korea builds a new Central Asian minerals partnership; and the Ideas desk asks whether morality can be decomposed into recurring social problems.

## Lead Story

### UN investigators say there are reasonable grounds to believe U.S. strikes in Iran amounted to war crimes

A UN-backed fact-finding mission says two U.S. strikes in Iran on February 28—on a primary school in Minab and a sports complex in Lamerd—were indiscriminate attacks that it has reasonable grounds to characterize as war crimes. The same mission says Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity during their crackdown on protests that began in December 2025.

**Why it matters:** The findings put alleged violations by both a foreign military and the Iranian state into one evidentiary record. They are conclusions of an independent UN-mandated fact-finding mission, not a court judgment. U.S. responsibility for Minab is supported by reporting on an internal military investigation; U.S. Central Command has denied carrying out the Lamerd strike, and the Pentagon has not released a final Minab investigation.

Source: Reuters — https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/un-mission-finds-grounds-believe-us-committed-war-crimes-iran-tehran-crimes-2026-09-17/

## Front Page

### South Sudan’s president dismissed the head of the election commission three months before the country’s first planned national vote

President Salva Kiir removed National Elections Commission chair Abednego Akok Kacuol and named Gabriel Bol Deng Majok as his replacement. No public explanation accompanied the dismissal. The commission had only recently begun training state committees for voter registration ahead of elections scheduled for December 22.

Source: Associated Press — https://apnews.com/article/1c2cadb70c3e22d120de41be7684d6e0

### South Korea and five Central Asian states created a recurring summit focused on critical minerals, energy and supply chains

Leaders from Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan met President Lee Jae Myung in Seoul for the first Korea–Central Asia summit. The meetings produced more than 70 agreements and memoranda and established a biennial leaders’ summit, with Kazakhstan due to host the next one in 2028.

Source: Reuters — https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/south-korea-puts-critical-minerals-energy-forefront-inaugural-central-asia-2026-09-16/

## In Case You Missed It

### Congo’s Ebola outbreak crossed 2,500 deaths in August while response workers were running short of money and facing attacks

On August 21, the UN’s senior Ebola coordinator said more than 2,500 people had died in the Democratic Republic of Congo’s Bundibugyo-virus outbreak and warned that available response funding was close to exhaustion. WHO data later confirmed continued expansion: by August 26, 5,794 cases and 2,786 deaths had been reported in Congo across six provinces.

Source: Reuters via News5 / TV5 Philippines — https://news.tv5.com.ph/foreign/read/ebola-outbreak-in-congo-spreading-exponentially-says-un-coordinator

## Ideas

### Can morality be broken into a small set of recurring social problems?

Oliver Scott Curry, Mark Alfano, Mark Brandt and Christine Pelican propose a framework of “moral molecules”: recurring combinations of basic cooperative concerns such as helping family, returning favors, coordinating groups, resolving conflict and respecting possession.

Source: Psyche — https://psyche.co/ideas/moral-molecules-a-new-theory-of-what-goodness-is-made-of

### A village library in the French Pyrenees has started a weekly hour of stories for local children

The library in Bedous, a small commune in the Aspe Valley, is holding Wednesday morning reading sessions through mid-October, with separate times for younger and older children. The program is free and organized through the local library and Haut-Béarn tourism network.

## Worth Your Time

### What if many metaphysical disputes are problems created by our own conceptual rules?

Timothy Williamson reviews Amie Thomasson’s *Rethinking Metaphysics*, which argues that philosophers often mistake questions about how our concepts work for discoveries about a deep structure of reality. Williamson presses the central difficulty: claiming there are no deep metaphysical truths can itself begin to look like a deep metaphysical claim.

Source: Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews — https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/rethinking-metaphysics/
