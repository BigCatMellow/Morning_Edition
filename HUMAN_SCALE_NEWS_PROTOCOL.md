# Morning Edition — Human-Scale News Protocol

## Purpose

Morning Edition should not imply that the world consists only of wars, elections, markets, disasters, institutional crises, scientific breakthroughs, and other large events.

Every day, billions of people are also living ordinary local lives: repairing a footbridge, looking for a missing sheep, opening a library room, arguing over a village sign, restoring a church clock, holding a school fair, rescuing an animal, harvesting a crop, reopening a neighborhood shop, or solving some small practical problem that matters greatly to the people nearby and hardly at all to the global news cycle.

This lane exists to preserve that sense of proportion.

Its job is not to make the reader laugh or to manufacture optimism. Its job is to briefly move the scale of the paper from the world system back down to a town, neighborhood, farm, school, family, club, local institution, or small community.

The editorial feeling should be:

> The world is large and serious, but it is also still made up of ordinary places where ordinary things are happening.

## Standard daily requirement

Normally include an **At Human Scale** section with **1–2 concise stories**.

Recommended section id: `at-human-scale`.

This is a recurring balance requirement, like `In Case You Missed It` and the durable Ideas lane. It should not disappear merely because the major-news sections are full.

It may be omitted only when a deliberate search fails to find a suitable item that can be verified. When omitted, `editorial_review` should say that the human-scale lane was searched and why no story was selected.

## What belongs here

Good candidates are real, low-stakes local stories whose significance is mostly local but whose ordinariness is itself useful.

Examples include:

- a farmer finding a missing sheep mixed into another herd;
- a village recovering a long-missing bell, sign, statue, or piece of local history;
- a school, library, volunteer group, fire company, garden club, or small museum solving a practical problem;
- a town restoring a footpath, clock, ferry, bridge, fountain, bus stop, or other piece of everyday infrastructure;
- an escaped animal, unusual migration, harvest, garden, weather oddity, or wildlife encounter that is genuinely local rather than sensational;
- a small community event with an unusually specific local story behind it;
- a local craftsperson, shop, farm, club, or institution doing something modest but revealing about everyday life;
- a municipal decision that is small in scope and interesting because of the people or place involved rather than because it is a proxy for national politics;
- a quiet human-interest story that would plausibly appear on page three of a good local newspaper rather than the front page of a national one.

The story does **not** need to be positive. It does need to be low-temperature and human-sized.

## What does not belong here

Do not use this lane for:

- national political stories disguised as local news;
- serious violent crime, death, disaster, or tragedy merely because it happened in a small town;
- culture-war stories;
- celebrity sightings;
- viral social-media posts without real reporting;
- novelty stories selected only because they are bizarre;
- corporate PR dressed up as community news;
- generic 'good news' or inspirational content;
- stories whose real purpose is to provoke outrage, sentimentality, or a laugh.

A useful test is: **Would this still be worth including if nobody found it funny, heartwarming, or shareable?** If yes, it may fit.

## Tone

Write these stories plainly and briefly.

Do not overstate their importance. Their value is precisely that they are **not** globally important.

Avoid language such as:

- 'restores your faith in humanity';
- 'the wholesome story we all needed';
- 'you won't believe';
- 'heartwarming';
- 'hilarious';
- 'only in...';
- forced moral lessons.

A better framing is specific and observational: what happened, where, who cared, and the small piece of local life it reveals.

`why_it_matters` may be unusually modest. It can simply explain why the item earns a place as a change of scale or as a glimpse of how a particular community works.

## Sourcing

Prefer genuinely local or regional sources when possible:

- small-town newspapers;
- local public radio or broadcasters;
- regional newspapers;
- local-language publications;
- municipal or community publications when independently verifiable;
- agricultural, rural, island, village, county, or neighborhood reporting.

Do not default to national aggregators when the original local story is accessible.

### Geographic priority: look beyond the United States first

**At Human Scale is deliberately not an American-local-news section.** Its strongest editorial use is to show ordinary life in places the rest of an English-language news diet rarely lets the reader inhabit.

The default expectation is therefore **non-U.S.**:

1. Begin the daily Human Scale search outside the United States.
2. Search multiple world regions rather than merely English-speaking countries: Europe, Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, South and East Asia, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Oceania, islands, rural regions, border communities, and other locally distinctive places.
3. Actively use original-language local reporting when practical. Translation effort is a feature of this lane, not a reason to fall back to U.S. material.
4. Consider a U.S. story only after a real non-U.S. search, and select it when it is clearly stronger, unusually revealing, or useful for geographic rotation—not simply because American local reporting is easier to find.
5. Avoid consecutive U.S. Human Scale selections when a credible non-U.S. alternative clears the bar.

As a rolling editorial target, **most Human Scale selections should come from outside the United States**. A useful benchmark is roughly **5 of every 7 daily selections non-U.S.**, without treating that number as a mechanical quota. If the recent run has become U.S.-heavy, the next search should deliberately correct the imbalance.

The section should also rotate within the non-U.S. world. Replacing 'small-town America' with only Britain, Canada, Australia, or another easy English-language source ecosystem would miss the purpose.

The goal is not exoticism. Do not select a place because it seems quaint or foreign. Select an ordinary local story because it gives the reader a truthful glimpse of everyday life somewhere they would otherwise be unlikely to encounter.

## Recency

Human-scale stories should usually come from roughly the **previous 1–7 days**. A slightly older item is acceptable when it is especially distinctive or when local reporting appeared slowly.

The lane is not intended as an archive feature; freshness helps it feel like a parallel view of the same world covered elsewhere in the edition.

## Placement and length

`At Human Scale` should normally appear **after the heavier current-news sections and before or near the final reflective material**. It functions as a tonal and perceptual reset.

Items should normally be shorter than Front Page or major subject-section stories. Most do not need a full Continue Reading treatment unless there is genuinely useful local history or context.

One strong item is better than two weak ones.

## Relationship to other sections

This lane is distinct from:

- **Outside the Bubble**, which is for intellectually surprising discovery;
- **Small Things Worth Knowing**, which can contain tools, archives, maps, datasets, facts, and small discoveries;
- **In Case You Missed It**, which rescues older consequential stories;
- **Ideas**, which is reflective or argumentative;
- conventional 'good news' sections, which select primarily for positive emotional effect.

A story can be interesting, delightful, or even funny, but those qualities are incidental rather than the selection rule.

## Editorial review

Each edition's `editorial_review` should include a `human_scale_check` or equivalent note stating either:

- that an `At Human Scale` item was selected, why it fits the lane, and its country/region;
- that the lane was deliberately searched but omitted because no candidate cleared the sourcing/tone bar.

The review should also watch the rolling geographic balance. If a U.S. item is selected, note that non-U.S. candidates were deliberately searched first. If recent Human Scale selections have become disproportionately U.S.-based or concentrated in a small set of English-speaking countries, record that drift and correct it in the next edition.

## Final test

Before publishing, ask:

> If someone read only the major-news sections, would they leave with the false impression that almost everything happening in the world is enormous, dangerous, political, or historically consequential?

Then ask a second question:

> Is this section actually widening the reader's picture of ordinary life around the world, or merely reproducing the easiest American and English-language local-news sources?

`At Human Scale` exists to correct both distortions with one small, true piece of ordinary life.
